By the Current River crossing
Lived Gorynych, dragon grim:
Three heads — every one was hungry,
Drank the current, drained it dim.

Comes a traveler — headlights fading,
Red light burning on the dash:
Battery sign — the Dragon's warning,
He won't let you get home fast.

Then his phone lit up and whispered:
"Dragon's got three heads, my friend.
First one's the belt — let's check it:
Is it whole, and tight, no bend?"

Second head — the battery clamps, all
Crusted white like winter snow:
Clean 'em up and snug 'em tight — and
Off that second head will go!

Three-head Dragon, Zmey Gorynych,
You won't steal my spark tonight!
Belt and clamps and alternator —
Three strikes, and I win the fight!

Third head's in the alternator —
"Pull the cable!" old-timers say —
No! Just meter it, motor running:
Under thirteen? Master's way.

Master swapped the worn-out brushes,
Put a new regulator in —
Headlights blazing bright as morning,
Dragon slunk back to his den.

Three-head Dragon, Zmey Gorynych,
You won't steal my spark tonight!
Belt and clamps and alternator —
Three strikes, and I win the fight!

Now the traveler fears no Dragon,
Knows his three heads, one by one;
Little light inside his pocket
Shines on every road he runs.

In plain words: what the tale means

The dragon's three heads
Three reasons it won't charge: belt, battery terminals, alternator.
Pull the cable
An old trick — pulling a battery cable with the engine running can fry the electronics. Don't.
Under thirteen
With the engine running, 13.8–14.5 V is normal; under 13 V — the alternator needs a master.
