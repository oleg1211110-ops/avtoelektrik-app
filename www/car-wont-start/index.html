Car won't start? Don't guess — triage it in three questions

A car that won't start feels like one big problem, but it's really three different ones, and a single question tells them apart: does the starter crank the engine? If it doesn't crank, the trouble is the starting/electrical side — the battery, grounds, starter, or a safety switch. If it cranks briskly but won't catch, the engine is missing fuel or spark, or security is blocking it. And if it starts for a second and dies, it's almost always the immobilizer. (This page is for gas engines — diesels have their own page.)

1. Quick read

Quick read: find your answer fast

Pick what happens when you turn the key. You learn which of the three families you're in — no-crank, cranks-no-start, or the immobilizer — and where to start.

2. The three things an engine needs — and the three questions

A gas engine needs exactly three things to fire: fuel, spark, and air with compression. Every no-start is one of those missing, or the engine not turning fast enough to use them. Three quick questions place almost any no-start: Does it crank? (brisk = battery/starter fine; slow/clicking/silent = the starting side). Does it have fuel and spark? (listen for the pump prime, check the ignition is alive). Is security blocking it? (a flashing Security/key light or a start-then-stall means the immobilizer cut fuel and spark).

3. Family 1 — it won't crank

The engine isn't turning over. Work cheap-and-common to rare: rapid clicking with the dash dimming = low voltage (a flat/weak battery, a bad ground, corroded terminals — clean and charge/jump; the most common no-start). Dash stays bright, one click or silence = the starter, its relay or fuse, or a safety switch (automatic fully in Park — try Neutral; manual needs the clutch; push-button needs the brake). Cranks slowly on a good battery = a tired starter or thick cold oil (cranking voltage shouldn't sag far below ~9.5–10 V — a ballpark).

Field trick (one start only)If the starter won't engage, tapping it with a light tool while a helper cranks can jar worn brushes into spinning for one more start — enough to reach a shop. It confirms the starter is the fault; it is not a repair — replace it right away.

4. Family 2 — it cranks but won't catch

The starter spins the engine fine, so the battery and starter are innocent. Find the missing ingredient. Fuel: key to ON (don't crank), listen for a 2-second pump prime — silence sends you to the fuel-pump fuse and relay (and an aftermarket alarm that may cut the pump wire); a hum only proves power reached the pump, so a pressure test proves the pump itself (the bad fuel pump page). Spark and signal: Check Engine should light at key-on; with a scan tool, rpm stuck at zero while cranking means no crankshaft signal — that's the crank sensor and often its chafed wiring; also suspect moisture after rain or a wash. Timing (rarer): if it stalled while driving and the starter suddenly spins easier and faster, suspect the timing belt/chain — no compression to push against.

5. Family 3 — it starts then dies, and the immobilizer

A start-then-stall is the immobilizer's signature: the car didn't recognize the key, let it start, then cut the fuel. Watch the Security/key light; confirm Check Engine lights at key-on (if not, the immobilizer may not be hearing the engine computer — a communication/power issue, not just the key); try the spare key and move phones, metal and a second chipped key away. Aftermarket alarm or remote start? These often immobilize by cutting the fuel-pump wire through a hidden bypass module whose wireless relay can fail with age — a silent pump or start-then-stall on a car with add-on security is often that module, not the factory immobilizer. A start-then-stall that began after a battery disconnect or throttle cleaning usually just needs a throttle/idle relearn.

We never help bypass a working immobilizerIf a key is lost, damaged or not recognized, the honest fixes are a new fob battery, clearing interference, checking the car's battery, and key programming with the vehicle's ownership documents — not defeating the anti-theft system.

6. What you can do yourself, step by step

  1. Ask the first question: does it crank? Brisk crank → go to the fuel/spark step. Slow, clicking or silent → the starting side.
  2. No crank: battery and connections first (dim dash + rapid clicking = low voltage — jump/charge, clean terminals and the ground). Bright dash + one click or silence = starter, relay/fuse, or a safety switch (Park/Neutral, clutch, brake). A flashing Security light can block the starter too.
  3. Cranks, no start: listen for the 2-second fuel-pump prime at key-on (silence = fuse, relay, or an alarm kill in the pump wire; a hum needs a pressure test). Then check spark — Check Engine on at key-on, watch rpm while cranking (zero = no crank signal), look at coils and plugs, and dry it out if it's wet from rain or a wash.
  4. Start-then-stall or Security flashing: work the immobilizer branch — spare key, move other fobs/phones/metal away, confirm Check Engine at key-on, and rule out an aftermarket alarm/bypass. A stall right after a battery disconnect or throttle cleaning usually just needs an idle relearn.
  5. Start from what was just done, and read the pattern. Recent repair → recheck what was touched. Hot-only → a heat-failing crank sensor. Cold-only → battery, thick oil, moisture. After rain/a wash → dry the ignition.
  6. Read the codes. An inexpensive OBD-II adapter reads stored codes even on a no-start — often the fastest way to point at the crank sensor, the pump circuit, or the immobilizer.

7. Common misdiagnoses to avoid

  • Buying a fuel pump because it "hums" — a hum only proves power reached it; only a pressure test proves the pump.
  • Replacing the starter when it's the battery or a ground — a dim dash and rapid clicking is low voltage.
  • Chasing fuel and spark while the Security light is flashing — that's the immobilizer blocking the start.
  • Replacing the crankshaft sensor when its wiring chafed at the drive belts — inspect the harness first.
  • Forgetting the simple safety switches — not fully in Park, clutch or brake not pressed.
  • Ignoring an aftermarket alarm's hidden kill in the fuel-pump wire, or a wireless relay that failed with age.
  • Missing the pattern — a hot-only, cold-only, or after-a-wash no-start is telling you the cause.

Questions

How do I figure out why my car won't start?

Ask three questions. Does it crank? No → battery, grounds, starter or a safety switch. Yes but won't catch → fuel, spark, or the immobilizer. Does it start then die, or is the Security light flashing → the immobilizer. That triage tells you which small set of parts to check instead of guessing.

My car cranks but won't start — what does that mean?

The starter and battery are fine; the engine is missing fuel or spark, or security is blocking it. Listen for the 2-second fuel-pump prime, confirm Check Engine lights up, and watch the Security light. No prime = the pump circuit; no spark/crank signal = ignition or the crankshaft sensor; Security flashing = the immobilizer.

The starter just clicks and won't crank — is it the battery or the starter?

Rapid clicking with the dash dimming is almost always the battery or a bad connection — charge or jump it and clean the terminals and ground. A single click with the dash staying bright points at the starter, its relay, or a safety switch (not in Park, clutch or brake not pressed).

Why does my car start and then stall immediately?

Usually the immobilizer: the car didn't recognize the key, allowed a brief start, then cut the fuel. Watch the Security light as it dies and try the spare key. If it began after a battery disconnect or throttle cleaning, it may just need an idle relearn instead.

How do I know if it's the immobilizer?

Look for a flashing Security or key symbol and a start-then-stall. Check that Check Engine still lights at key-on (if not, the immobilizer may not be hearing the computer). Try the spare key and remove other fobs, phones and metal from near it. An aftermarket alarm with remote start is a common hidden cause.

Why won't my car start only when hot, only in the cold, or after a car wash?

Patterns name the cause. Hot-only often means a heat-failing crankshaft sensor. Cold-only is usually the battery losing punch and oil thickening, sometimes moisture or fuel. After rain or a wash points at moisture in the ignition — dry the coils, plug wires and connectors and try again.
