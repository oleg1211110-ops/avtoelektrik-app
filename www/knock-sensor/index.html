Knock sensor: symptoms and how to test it yourself

The knock sensor sits on the engine block and “listens” for knock: when it detects it, the ECU retards the ignition timing. If the sensor or its wire is faulty, the ECU keeps the timing retarded just in case — the engine loses power, hesitates on acceleration, uses more fuel, and the Check Engine light comes on. You may also hear a metallic pinging under acceleration — that’s the knock itself.

2. How to test it, step by step

  1. Read the codes: P0325 — knock sensor circuit, P0326 — signal out of range, P0327 and P0328 — low and high input. Knock sensor codes are common on Toyotas.
  2. Find the sensor: it is bolted to the engine block — usually under the intake manifold or between the cylinders, and you often have to remove the manifold to reach it. Inspect the connector and the wire: a common failure is a wire broken right at the connector and insulation cracked by heat.
  3. Scan tool: on a warm engine, ignition timing at idle is usually 0–20°, and at 2500 rpm with no load 25–40°. If the scan tool shows knock retard, it should normally be about 0°. If timing is always low, the ECU is playing it safe: the sensor, the wiring or bad gas.
  4. Tap test: at warm idle, gently tap the block next to the sensor (not the sensor itself) with a small hammer or a wrench. The live data should show timing retard or a spike in the sensor signal. No reaction means the sensor or the wiring is bad.
  5. Multimeter or oscilloscope: unplug the connector and hook the meter to the sensor pins, with the multimeter on AC millivolts. Tap lightly next to the sensor: a good one produces voltage pulses and the reading jumps. An oscilloscope will show a spike for every tap.
  6. Resistance: with the sensor unplugged, measure between the pins and from each pin to the sensor body. Near 0 Ω or a short to the body means the sensor is bad. The spec varies widely by model — from a few kΩ to hundreds of kΩ or more, so check the manual for your engine.
  7. On installation the sensor must sit flat on clean block metal with its whole mounting face — no washers, paint or sealant. Torque it to the manual spec, usually about 20 N·m (15 lb·ft). Over- or under-tightened, it either “hears” knock poorly or gives false signals.
  8. If pinging and timing retard showed up after a fill-up and the sensor checks out fine, the cause may be poor-quality gas. Fill up with gas of the right octane rating at a station you trust.
Oleg’s tipOften the problem isn’t the sensor itself but the wiring under the intake manifold. Many people replace the sensor right away and the cause stays — so I check the wire and the connector first.
