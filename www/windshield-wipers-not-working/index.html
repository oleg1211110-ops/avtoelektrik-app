Windshield wipers not working: what to check yourself

2. What to check yourself, step by step

  1. Check the wiper fuse — labeled WIPER, RR WIPER or RR WIP on the fuse box lid. In winter, first check whether the blade is frozen to the glass: don’t switch on frozen wipers, warm up the glass first.
  2. Switch on the rear wiper and listen at the trunk lid. Motor hums but the blade doesn’t move — the shaft has seized or the arm splines are stripped. Motor is silent — no power, a faulty switch or the motor itself.
  3. With the wiper switched on, measure the voltage at the motor connector: about 12 V. If it’s there but the motor doesn’t turn, replace the motor. If not, check the stalk switch and the wiring.
  4. Inspect the wires in the boot between the body and the trunk lid: with the wiper on, slowly open and close the lid. If the wiper jerks or stops, a wire has broken inside the boot.
  5. If it leaves streaks and smears, the rubber has lost its flexibility, cracked or worn down. Replace the blades every 6–12 months and choose ones made for your car and the season: cheap universal blades wear out faster.
  6. Before replacing the blades, wash the glass and wipe the rubber with a soapy sponge: tree sap, tar and poplar fluff cause squeaking and missed spots. If the blade doesn’t press well at the edges, the arm spring has weakened or the blade is at the wrong angle.
  7. If the wipers move in jerks, slowly, or stop mid-glass, the motor, transmission or linkage is worn. The wiper drive needs repair.
Oleg’s tipIf on a car with a liftgate the rear wiper, plate light, rear defroster or camera all stop working at once, the first thing I check is the wiring in the boot between the body and the liftgate — that’s where it breaks most often.

Washer not working

When a washer doesn’t work, I check the fluid level, the pump motor on the reservoir, the connectors and the power. Very often it’s something simple: an empty reservoir or corroded contacts. Operate the washer and listen for the pump on the reservoir. If it’s silent, check its connector for corrosion and the voltage there with the stalk held: about 12 V. If there’s voltage but the pump stays silent, replace the pump.

Where is the washer fluid reservoir?

The washer reservoir is almost always under the hood, near a fender or headlight. Usually one reservoir serves both the windshield and the rear window: fluid reaches the rear nozzle through a hose running the length of the car. Open the hood and look for a white or translucent plastic reservoir with a blue, yellow or black cap. The cap shows a windshield symbol with water spray. It opens without tools.
