2. Where to find the VIN

  1. At the bottom of the windshield on the driver’s side — read it from outside.
  2. On the sticker in the driver’s door jamb.
  3. On the registration, the title and the insurance card.
  4. In the engine control unit: an inexpensive ELM327 adapter and the free AvtoElektrik PRO app read it through the OBD-II port. Buying a used car? The VIN in the computer should match the one on the body and the documents.

3. Model year codes (position 10)

A = 2010 · B = 2011 · C = 2012 · D = 2013 · E = 2014 · F = 2015 · G = 2016 · H = 2017 · J = 2018 · K = 2019 · L = 2020 · M = 2021 · N = 2022 · P = 2023 · R = 2024 · S = 2025 · T = 2026

Letters I, O, Q, U, Z and the digit 0 are never used for the year. Codes repeat every 30 years (A was also 1980); for cars and light trucks a letter in position 7 means 2010 or later. 49 CFR Part 565.

Questions

Is VIN decoding free?

Yes. This page uses NHTSA’s official vPIC database (the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration): free, no sign-up. It returns what the manufacturer reported for that VIN: year, make, model, engine, body, plant.

How do I check recalls by VIN?

This page lists the recalls NHTSA has for the model and year. Whether a recall has already been fixed on your exact car is shown by the official VIN lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls or by a dealer of that brand.

What does the 10th digit of a VIN mean?

The model year: A = 2010, B = 2011 … L = 2020, M = 2021, N = 2022, P = 2023, R = 2024, S = 2025, T = 2026. The letters I, O, Q, U, Z and the digit 0 are not used. The codes repeat every 30 years; for cars and light trucks a letter in position 7 means 2010 or later.

Where is the VIN on my car?

At the bottom of the windshield on the driver’s side (seen from outside), on the sticker in the driver’s door jamb, on the registration, title and insurance card, and often in the engine bay.

Can a VIN decoder show accidents or previous owners?

No. A free decoder shows how the car was built. Accident, title and ownership history come from paid vehicle history reports.

What is the check digit?

Position 9 on vehicles built for North America. It is calculated from the other 16 characters, so a typo almost always makes it not match — this page checks it for you.
