First tell whether the light is steady or flashing, check the simple things yourself, then read the code. Most causes are cheap to check before you pay for a repair.

2. What you can check yourself

  1. Gas cap: tighten it until it clicks. A loose or cracked cap is a classic cause of EVAP codes (P0455, P0457).
  2. Think about what changed: a fill-up at a new station, a recent repair, a disconnected battery or a jump start.
  3. Look under the hood for an unplugged connector or a cracked vacuum hose near the engine.
  4. Read the code with an inexpensive ELM327 adapter and the free AvtoElektrik PRO app, or ask an auto parts store to read it.
  5. Open the page for your code: causes are listed from the most likely and cheapest to check.

Questions

Can I keep driving with the Check Engine light on?

If it is steady and the car drives normally, you can usually drive carefully to read the code and plan a repair. If it is flashing, there is a severe misfire: ease off, avoid hard acceleration and long trips, and get it checked soon — unburned fuel can overheat and ruin the catalytic converter.

Can a loose gas cap turn on the Check Engine light?

Yes. A loose or worn gas cap is a common cause of EVAP codes such as P0455 or P0457. Tighten the cap until it clicks; the light may go off by itself after several days of driving.

Will clearing the code fix the problem?

No. Clearing only turns the light off until the computer detects the fault again. It also resets the readiness monitors, so the car will not pass a smog check until they are complete again.

How can I read the code myself?

Plug an inexpensive ELM327 adapter into the OBD-II port under the dashboard and use the free AvtoElektrik PRO app (Android). Many auto parts stores in the US will also read the code for free.
