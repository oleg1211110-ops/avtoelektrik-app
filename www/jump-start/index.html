How to jump start a car safely

Jump-starting is safe if you keep to the order of the cables. Reversed polarity or a spark at the battery can burn out control units, and battery gas can ignite. Practice the order below, then do it for real.

2. Step by step

  1. Park the cars close, but not touching. Switch off the donor’s engine, and turn off the ignition and everything you don’t need in both cars.
  2. Check the voltage: 12 V is jumped only from 12 V, and 24 V only from 24 V.
  3. Red cable: first to the plus (+) of the dead battery, then to the donor’s plus.
  4. Black cable: first to the donor’s minus (−), then to clean, unpainted metal on your car’s engine, away from the battery. Not to the dead battery’s minus: a spark at the battery is dangerous.
  5. If the battery is in the trunk or under a seat, there are usually jump-start terminals under the hood: a plus post under a cover and a ground bolt.
  6. Start the donor and let it run for 5–10 minutes: the dead battery will take a little charge.
  7. Start your car: crank no longer than 10 seconds, with a 30-second pause between attempts. If it won’t start after 3–4 attempts, don’t torture the starter — the cause may not be the battery.
  8. Once it starts, remove the cables in reverse order: black from your car, black from the donor, red from the donor, red from your car. The cable ends must not touch each other or the body.
  9. A drive won’t fully recharge the battery: charge it with a charger and find out why it went flat.

3. With a jump starter (booster pack)

A portable jump starter is connected the same way: red to the plus (+) of the battery, black to bare metal on the engine — and only then switched on. In winter keep it inside the car: lithium jump starters get noticeably weaker in the cold.

Trucks on 24 V are jumped only from 24 V. The battery disconnect switch on the dead vehicle must be on.

4. Why did the battery die?

Once a light left on — just charge the battery. Dies again after a night or a few days parked — usually some unit doesn’t go to sleep (an aftermarket alarm, stereo or dash cam first). Questions

Which jumper cable goes on first?

Red first: to the plus (+) of the dead battery, then to the plus of the donor car. Then black: to the donor’s minus (−), and last to clean, unpainted metal on the engine of the car you are starting.

Why not put the last black clamp on the dead battery’s minus?

The last connection can spark, and a battery gives off gas that can ignite. Engine metal away from the battery is just as good an electrical ground and much safer.

How long should the donor car run before I try to start?

Let the donor run for 5–10 minutes: the dead battery takes a little charge. Then crank no longer than 10 seconds, with a 30-second pause between attempts.

How long should I drive after a jump start?

A drive won’t fully recharge the battery. Charge it with a battery charger and find out why it died: a light left on, an old battery, or something draining it while the car is parked.

Can I jump a 24 V truck from a car?

No. 12 V is jumped only from 12 V, and 24 V only from 24 V. On a truck, the battery disconnect switch of the dead vehicle must be on.

Can jump-starting damage my car?

Yes, if the polarity is reversed or the clamps spark at the battery: fuses and control units can burn out. Keep to the cable order and keep the clamp ends from touching each other or the body.
