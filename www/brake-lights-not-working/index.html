    2. What to check yourself

    1. Check the lights: at night back up close to a garage door or a shop window and watch the reflection, or wedge the pedal down with a stick against the seat.
    2. None work, including the third one — check the STOP (or BRAKE) fuse: how to check a fuse.
    3. Fuse fine — the brake light switch at the top of the pedal: it should click when you press the pedal. If cruise control also stopped working, or a push-button car won’t start with the pedal pressed, it’s the switch.
    4. One side out — pull the bulb: in a two-filament bulb the brake filament breaks first. Replace it with the same number on the base; clean a green, corroded socket.
    5. One side dim, or the turn signal glows with the brakes on — a poor ground: clean the ground point and the connector at that taillight.
    6. Lights stay on — look at the pedal arm for a missing rubber stopper that presses the switch. Fix it soon: the lights drain the battery overnight.
    Don’t drive without brake lightsThe car behind won’t see you braking. Until it’s fixed, use the hazard lights when you slow down sharply — and fix it as soon as you can.

    Questions

    Why are none of my brake lights working?

    All of them out at once — including the third, center light — is rarely the bulbs. Check the brake light (STOP) fuse and the brake light switch at the top of the pedal. If cruise control also stopped working, or a push-button car won’t start, the switch is the prime suspect: they use the same signal.

    Why does one brake light not work?

    Usually the bulb: in a two-filament bulb (taillight and brake light in one) the brighter brake filament breaks first. If the bulb is fine — a corroded socket or a poor ground on that side.

    Why do my brake lights stay on?

    The brake light switch isn’t being released. Very often a small rubber stopper on the pedal arm has crumbled and fallen out, so the switch never gets pressed back in. Fix it soon — the lights also drain the battery overnight.

    How can I check my brake lights by myself?

    At night back up close to a garage door or a shop window and watch the reflection while you press the pedal. Or wedge the pedal down with a stick against the seat and walk around the car.

    Why is the third brake light out but the others work?

    Most third brake lights are LED units: when it’s dark but gets 12 V at its connector with the pedal pressed, the unit has failed and is replaced as a whole. No voltage — look at the wiring, often in the trunk lid or tailgate hinge where it bends.
