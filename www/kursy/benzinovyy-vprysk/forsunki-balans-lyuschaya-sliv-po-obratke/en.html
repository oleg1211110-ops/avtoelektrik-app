Injectors: balance, leaking, return back-leak

A dirty or leaking injector richens the mixture and causes misfires. How cylinder balance and diesel return back-leak are tested — and why you don't replace injectors blind.

The injector meters the fuel. If it leaks, or is dirty and underdelivers, the mixture in that one cylinder suffers: hence both excess fuel use and misfires. Injectors are tested per cylinder, not replaced as a set just in case.

Gasoline injectors

A leaking or dripping injector richens the mixture and causes misfires — fuel use and rough running. A dirty one, the opposite, underdelivers. You test with a cylinder balance and a performance test: watch how RPM and trims change as each cylinder is cut, and whether one is off.

Diesel injectors — return back-leak

On a diesel the main test is measuring return back-leak per cylinder. A big leak means a worn or leaking injector: it richens, smokes and raises fuel use. Back-leak is measured with graduated vials or the scanner's built-in test.

How to check: yourself and the shop

The driver notices the symptoms: rough running, smoky exhaust, a fuel smell, hunting idle. Cylinder balance and injector back-leak are checked by a shop with a scanner and on a bench. On gasoline, always check fuel pressure and a vacuum leak before the injectors — often the blamed injector turns out to be low pressure or extra air.

A balance that's off on one cylinder with steady pressure and no vacuum leak is a good reason to look at that cylinder's injector and plug. One cylinder stands out — look for a local cause, don't replace everything.

Don't replace injectors blind for prevention. Fuel pressure, vacuum leak and cylinder balance first — often the cause isn't the injector, and a set is expensive.

Key points
  • A leaking injector — rich, misfires, fuel use; a dirty one underdelivers. Tested per cylinder, not as a set.
  • Gasoline — cylinder balance and a performance test; diesel — return back-leak per cylinder.
  • Pressure and vacuum leak first, injectors after: often the blamed injector is something else.

Try it yourself: injector return back-leak

Move the injector wear and watch the back-leak. Then switch on a worn one (big leak, rich) and a leaking one, and restore a healthy one.

Check yourself

What does a dripping (leaking) injector do to fuel use?
  • The mixture goes lean
  • The mixture goes rich, misfires and fuel use
  • Nothing
  • Rail pressure drops

Answer: The mixture goes rich, misfires and fuel use — A leaking injector delivers extra fuel — a rich mixture, misfires and excess fuel use in that cylinder.

How are diesel injectors checked for wear?
  • By sound
  • By measuring return back-leak per cylinder
  • By replacing all of them at once
  • By the injector's color

Answer: By measuring return back-leak per cylinder — A big return back-leak on a cylinder is a sign of a worn or leaking injector. It's the main diesel test.

On gasoline, a complaint of fuel use and rough running. What to check before replacing injectors?
  • Replace the injectors as a set right away
  • Fuel pressure, a vacuum leak and cylinder balance
  • Replace the catalytic converter
  • Pour in an additive

Answer: Fuel pressure, a vacuum leak and cylinder balance — Often the blamed injector turns out to be low pressure or a vacuum leak. Pressure and balance first, then a verdict on the injector.
