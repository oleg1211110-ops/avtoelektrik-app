The fuel system: injection types and what to check

Port, direct, combined, single-point and diesel: what pressure is normal and what to check in live data.

Engines deliver fuel in different ways — each type has its own pressure and its own things to watch in live data. Let's go through five.

Port (multipoint) injection

The injector sits in the intake port ahead of the valve. Pressure is low: about 6 bar on Bosch systems, 3–4 bar on older ones. Watch fuel trims within ±~10%, the lambda swinging, and steady pressure. This is the most common gasoline injection.

Direct injection (GDI, FSI, TSI)

The injector sprays straight into the cylinder, with pressure in two stages: a lift feed from the tank up to 6 bar and a high-pressure rail — tens of bar at idle, up to 350 bar under load (Bosch). The key parameter is commanded vs actual rail pressure: they must track together. If actual lags, supply is short — the metering valve or the high-pressure pump.

Direct-injection injectors run on a boosted 60–90 V — you can't test them with an ordinary multimeter. A common problem is carbon on the intake valves: the fuel doesn't wash them.

Combined injection (for example Toyota D-4S)

It has both port and direct injection at once. Cold and at light load the port system works — also washing the intake valves; under load the direct side kicks in. Live data shows which injector group is active — that's how you hunt misfires.

Single-point (central) injection

One injector above the throttle feeds the whole intake, with low pressure — about 1 bar. There's little live data: rpm, lambda, throttle position, temperature. Old cars — diagnosis is mostly hands-on: pressure, spark, injector.

Diesel (Common Rail)

A common high-pressure rail: about 300–400 bar at idle, up to 1800–2500 bar under load. Commanded and actual pressure must match again. Check the pressure regulator on the injection pump, the injector balance and correction per cylinder, and the return. We check and restore the system — we don't disable it.

On a diesel, don't open the high-pressure lines sooner than 10 minutes after shutdown — the pressure there is dangerous.

Key points
  • Each injection has its own pressure: port ~6 bar, direct up to 350 bar, diesel up to 2500 bar.
  • On direct injection and diesel, the main thing is to compare commanded and actual rail pressure.
  • Combined injection shows which injector group is working; single-point is an old, simple system.

Check yourself

On a gasoline direct-injection engine, actual rail pressure lags well behind commanded under load. What's most likely?
  • Everything's fine, that's how it should be
  • Supply is short: the metering valve or the high-pressure pump
  • Carbon on the intake valves
  • A weak battery

Answer: Supply is short: the metering valve or the high-pressure pump — Commanded and actual rail pressure must track together. Lagging under load is a sign of insufficient high-pressure supply.

Which fuel pressure is closest to normal for port (multipoint) injection?
  • About 1 bar
  • About 6 bar
  • About 150 bar
  • Up to 2000 bar

Answer: About 6 bar — Port is a low-pressure system, about 6 bar on Bosch (3–4 on older ones). ~1 bar is single-point; hundreds to thousands of bar is direct injection and diesel.
