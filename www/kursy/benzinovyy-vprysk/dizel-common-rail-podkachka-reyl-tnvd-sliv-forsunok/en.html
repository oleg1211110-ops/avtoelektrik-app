Diesel Common Rail: lift pump, rail, HP pump, injector back-leak

Two pressure circuits: a 4–7 bar lift pump and a rail up to 2000+ bar. What to check first, how to read pressure on the scanner, and why the high-pressure pump is replaced, not repaired.

A Common Rail diesel has two pressure circuits. The low one is the lift (supply) that feeds fuel from the tank to the high-pressure pump. The high one is the rail, from which the injectors spray into the cylinders. You diagnose from simple to complex, and you always check the lift first.

Low-pressure circuit (lift) — check it first

Lift pressure is roughly 4-7 bar, and at idle not below about 4.0 bar. Low lift means air in the system, hard starting and hesitation. The causes are simple and cheap: a clogged filter, air drawn in at fittings and quick-connects, little fuel in the tank. Even a small air leak can stop the high-pressure pump.

The rail and its pressure

Rail pressure is roughly: about 250-350 bar at idle, and about 1400-2000 bar and above at full load depending on the system generation. Pressure is controlled by the regulator (the metering valve or DRV) and the rail pressure sensor. Codes: P0087 — pressure low, P0088 — high. Commanded and actual rail pressure show on the scanner.

The high-pressure pump

A worn high-pressure pump gives pressure drops, hard starting, power loss up top, and fuel diluting the oil. The pump isn't repaired — it's replaced. But before replacing you must check the drive, rollers and cam, flush the system, change the filter and prime it: otherwise the same cause kills the new pump.

Injectors — return back-leak

As on any diesel, injectors are checked by measuring return back-leak per cylinder: a big leak is a worn or leaking injector, hence smoke and fuel use. That's a big topic of its own — in detail in the Diesel Fuel Equipment course.

Do-it-yourself-first for a diesel with a 'the pump won't prime' complaint: check whether there's fuel in the tank, whether the filter is filled correctly, and whether the connections are airtight. In Oleg's experience, air in the system and a leak at quick-connects is often the real cause, not a dead pump.

Don't replace the high-pressure pump without finding and fixing the cause of its wear (dirt, water, an air leak). Otherwise the new pump fails the same way. And don't go into high rail pressure with the engine running — it's dangerous.

Key points
  • Two circuits: a 4-7 bar lift (not below ~4.0 at idle) and a rail of ~250-350 bar at idle, up to 1400-2000+ under load.
  • Check the lift first: filter, air leak, fuel in the tank. A small leak stops the high-pressure pump.
  • Rail pressure — on the scanner (P0087 low / P0088 high). The pump isn't repaired, it's replaced, but you fix the cause of wear first.

Try it yourself: rail pressure

Raise the RPM and watch rail pressure. Then switch on low supply (pump/lift wear, P0087) and a stuck regulator (high, P0088) and restore normal.

Check yourself

Which circuit do you start pressure diagnosis with on Common Rail?
  • The high-pressure rail
  • The low-pressure lift circuit (4-7 bar)
  • The injectors
  • The turbo

Answer: The low-pressure lift circuit (4-7 bar) — You always check the lift first: it's cheaper and more often at fault — filter, air leak, fuel in the tank. A small leak stops the high-pressure pump.

The scanner shows code P0087 (low rail pressure). What should you NOT do first?
  • Check the lift and the filter
  • Replace the high-pressure pump right away without finding the cause
  • Check for an air leak
  • Look at commanded and actual rail pressure

Answer: Replace the high-pressure pump right away without finding the cause — The high-pressure pump is replaced last and only after fixing the cause of wear. Cheap stuff first: lift, filter, air leak.

Why does the high-pressure pump need the system flushed and the filter changed before replacement?
  • For looks
  • Otherwise the cause of wear (dirt, water, an air leak) kills the new pump
  • It's faster that way
  • It's not necessary

Answer: Otherwise the cause of wear (dirt, water, an air leak) kills the new pump — A new pump will fail the same way if you don't remove the cause: dirt, water, an air leak. So flushing, the filter and checking the drive are mandatory.
