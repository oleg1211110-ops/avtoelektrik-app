Lean and rich mixture: the catalyst is not to blame

STFT/LTFT fuel trims are the main mixture indicator. What lean and rich mean, how the O2 sensor, MAF, coolant temperature sensor and vacuum leaks fit in, and why the catalytic converter is usually not to blame.

Mixture codes are P0171/P0174 (lean) and P0172/P0175 (rich). They are a fuel-system fault: air, fuel pressure, injectors. The oxygen sensor is only a messenger — it tells the ECU what the mixture is and is almost never the cause itself. The catalyst has nothing to do with the mixture — you don't replace it for a mixture code.

How to read fuel trims

A scan tool shows two trims: short-term (STFT) jumps in real time, long-term (LTFT) is what the ECU has learned. Add them up. A big plus means the ECU is adding fuel — the mixture is lean; a big minus means it's removing fuel — the mixture is rich. The code sets when long-term trim hits about ±25 %; normally it stays within ±10 %.

Lean mixture (P0171/P0174)

Too much air or too little fuel. In order: air (vacuum) leak (hoses, intake gasket, vacuum lines, PCV valve — best found with smoke); an exhaust leak ahead of the front sensor; the MAF under-reading; low fuel pressure (weak pump, clogged filter); dirty injectors.

Rich mixture (P0172/P0175)

Too much fuel or too little air. High fuel pressure (regulator or a blocked return line); leaking injectors; the EVAP purge valve stuck open; the coolant temp sensor reading cold so the ECU adds fuel; a clogged air filter or the MAF over-reading.

A trick that saves an hour: compare idle and 2500 rpm. Strongly lean at idle but near zero at 2500 means an air leak. Lean the same everywhere means the MAF is under-reading or fuel pressure is low.

The catalyst myth (P0420)

P0420 means 'catalyst efficiency below threshold.' The ECU compares the sensor before and after the catalyst: with a good catalyst the rear sensor barely moves (about 0.6–0.7 V) while the front swings fast 0.1↔0.9 V. If the rear swings almost like the front, the catalyst isn't storing oxygen. But P0420 often does not mean a dead catalyst: the cause may be a tired rear sensor, an exhaust leak, or an upstream problem — misfires and a rich mixture. Those are exactly what burn the catalyst: unburned fuel ignites inside it and melts the honeycomb. So even when the catalyst really is dead, the root cause is upstream.

Don't replace the catalyst for P0420 without fixing the upstream cause (misfires, rich mixture, exhaust leak, tired rear sensor) — the new one will burn out the same way. That's the answer to 'we replaced the catalyst and it didn't help.'

Sensors that distort the mixture

A dirty MAF overreads airflow — the ECU adds more, hence fuel use; codes P0100-P0103, cleaned with a special MAF cleaner, never touching the element by hand. A coolant temp sensor (ECT) that reads low makes the ECU hold the rich warm-up mixture longer — a common, cheap culprit, code P0128. Warmed-up references: ECT about 90 C, a narrowband O2 sensor switching quickly 0.1 to 0.9 V.

A vacuum leak — a common cause of excess fuel use

Air that gets in past the MAF (a cracked hose, a slipped clamp, a vacuum line, the PCV valve) is unseen by the ECU — the mixture goes lean and it adds fuel. The scanner signature: trims swing positive, more at idle than off idle; often P0171/P0174. The MAP sensor at idle should drop with vacuum to about 30-35 kPa (in Almaty, key on and engine off, it reads about 90 kPa because of the altitude); if it doesn't drop at idle, look for a leak. The most precise way is a smoke machine.

Key points
  • A mixture code is a fuel-system problem (air, pressure, injectors), not the catalyst or the sensor.
  • Fuel trims: a big plus means lean, a big minus means rich; the limit is about ±25 %.
  • Replace the catalyst for P0420 last — the upstream cause comes first, or the new one will die the same way.

Check yourself

Long-term fuel trim is steady at about +22 % both at idle and at speed. What does this tell you?
  • The mixture is rich, the ECU is removing fuel
  • The mixture is lean, the ECU is adding fuel — look for a leak, pressure, MAF
  • Time to replace the catalyst
  • The rear oxygen sensor is faulty

Answer: The mixture is lean, the ECU is adding fuel — look for a leak, pressure, MAF — A big plus means the ECU is adding fuel, so the mixture is lean. The cause is in the fuel system: an air leak, low pressure, an under-reading MAF. The catalyst has nothing to do with it.

P0420 is on. What do you check first?
  • Immediately buy and fit a catalyst
  • Misfires and mixture, exhaust leak and rear sensor — and only then a verdict on the catalyst
  • Replace both oxygen sensors
  • Clean the throttle body

Answer: Misfires and mixture, exhaust leak and rear sensor — and only then a verdict on the catalyst — P0420 is often not about a dead catalyst. First clear the misfires and mixture (they burn the catalyst), then check the exhaust and rear sensor. Replacing the catalyst without fixing the cause burns out the new one too.

Trim is strongly positive at idle but near zero at 2500 rpm. What is most likely?
  • A clogged catalyst
  • An air leak
  • Leaking injectors
  • High fuel pressure

Answer: An air leak — An air leak shows up most at idle: at 2500 rpm the airflow is large and the leak 'drowns' in it. Exactly this idle-vs-2500 trim difference points to a leak.
