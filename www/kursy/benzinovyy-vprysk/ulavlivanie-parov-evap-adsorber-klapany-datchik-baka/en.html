Evaporative emissions (EVAP): canister, valves, tank sensor

How the EVAP system is built (canister, purge and vent valves, fuel-tank pressure sensor, leak-detection pump), why its codes usually don't affect driving — and when they do, and what to check yourself.

EVAP is the fuel-vapor capture system. Gasoline evaporates in the tank, and so the vapor doesn't escape to the atmosphere it is collected in a charcoal canister and later burned in the engine. It is a purely emissions system: almost all its faults give only a check engine light and don't affect driving — except for one case below.

What it is made of

The charcoal canister collects vapor from the tank. The purge valve, between the canister and the intake, opens to pull vapor into the engine. The vent valve connects the canister to the atmosphere through a filter and lets fresh air in. The fuel-tank pressure sensor (FTP) watches pressure and vacuum in the tank. Some cars have a leak-detection pump. And the gas cap is part of the system too (a bad seal is a common cause of a code).

How it works

Engine off — vapor collects in the canister. Warm engine — the ECU opens the purge valve, intake vacuum pulls vapor from the canister to be burned, and the vent valve is open to let air in. To test for leaks the ECU closes the vent and watches the tank-pressure sensor to see whether vacuum or pressure holds (or runs the leak-detection pump). If it doesn't hold, there is a leak somewhere and a leak code is set.

Codes and symptoms

Most often just the light, no effect on driving (it is vapor control). P0455 — large leak (often a loose or bad gas cap), P0456 — small, P0442 — medium, P0446 — vent valve, P0443/P0496 — purge valve, P0451–P0453 — tank-pressure sensor. The codes of one leak-detection unit (P043E, P043F, P2401, P2402, P2419) are read together. The case where EVAP really affects driving: the purge valve stuck open — extra vapor goes into the intake constantly: unstable idle, hard starting, a fuel smell. Hard to refuel (the tank 'won't take' fuel) — the vent is blocked.

What you can do yourself

First the gas cap — a common culprit for P0455: check the gasket, close it until it clicks, replace it if in doubt. Inspect the EVAP hoses for cracks and pop-offs. Check the purge and vent valves: they stick — clean or replace, and make sure the purge valve holds closed (doesn't pass vapor with the engine off). Check the tank-pressure sensor and its connector. The leak itself is best found with a smoke machine. There is no rush on the road — but a fuel smell and a purge valve stuck open are best not left.

Of the whole EVAP system, essentially the only thing that really affects driving is a purge valve stuck open: it adds vapor to the intake, so the engine runs rough and starts hard. Check that it holds closed before you look for the cause elsewhere.
Key points
  • EVAP catches fuel vapor in the canister and later burns it in the intake: purge valve, vent valve, tank-pressure sensor, sometimes a leak-detection pump, the gas cap.
  • Almost all EVAP codes don't affect driving (vapor control); the exception is a purge valve stuck open: rough running, hard start, a fuel smell.
  • Yourself: first the gas cap (P0455), then the hoses, the purge/vent valves and the pressure sensor; the leak is found with smoke.

Check yourself

What does the EVAP system do?
  • Increases power
  • Catches fuel vapor from the tank in the canister and later burns it in the intake, without releasing it to the atmosphere
  • Cools the engine
  • Cleans the oil

Answer: Catches fuel vapor from the tank in the canister and later burns it in the intake, without releasing it to the atmosphere — EVAP is an emissions system: gasoline vapor from the tank collects in the charcoal canister, then on a warm engine it is pulled through the purge valve into the intake and burned. No vapor escapes to the atmosphere.

The check engine light is on, code P0455, the car drives fine, sometimes a fuel smell. Where do you start?
  • Replace the canister right away
  • With the gas cap (a common cause of a large leak), then the hoses and valves; usually no effect on driving
  • Replace the catalytic converter
  • Replace the pump

Answer: With the gas cap (a common cause of a large leak), then the hoses and valves; usually no effect on driving — P0455 is a large leak, and the most common and cheap culprit is the gas cap (gasket, not fully closed). Then you look at the hoses and the purge/vent valves. This is vapor control — usually no effect on driving, so you can work it out calmly.
