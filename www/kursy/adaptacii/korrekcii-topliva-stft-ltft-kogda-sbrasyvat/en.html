Fuel trims (STFT/LTFT): when to reset

The unit trims fuel delivery to hold the right mixture: short-term (STFT) and long-term (LTFT) trims. After a mixture repair they are reset so the unit learns from a sound baseline. But a reset without fixing the cause is pointless.

To hold stoichiometry the unit constantly trims fuel delivery by the oxygen sensor. The short-term trim (STFT) swings quickly back and forth around zero; the long-term (LTFT) remembers a steady offset. That is the learned fuel adaptation.

What the trims show

A large positive — the unit is adding fuel (it sees lean: a leak, weak pressure, a MAF reading low). A large negative — it’s cutting fuel (rich). A mixture code (P0171/P0172) sets when the long-term trim hits about ±25 %; normal is within ±10 %. Trims are diagnostics, not just an adaptation.

When and why to reset

After the cause is repaired (leak fixed, injectors replaced, MAF cleaned) the long-term trim still holds the old “wrong” offset. It is reset so the unit learns again from the right baseline, and you drive a cycle to relearn.

Resetting trims does not fix the mixture. Reset it with a live leak or a weak pump and the trim drifts back to ±25 % and the code returns. Cause first, then reset.

Key points
  • STFT is the fast trim around zero, LTFT is the remembered steady offset; that is the fuel adaptation.
  • A large positive = adding (lean), a large negative = cutting (rich); a code at about ±25 %, normal ±10 %.
  • Reset only AFTER the cause is fixed, then a drive cycle; a reset on its own doesn’t fix the mixture.

Check yourself

What is the long-term fuel trim (LTFT)?
  • A mileage counter
  • A steady offset of fuel delivery the unit has learned to hold the right mixture
  • The oil level
  • The exhaust temperature

Answer: A steady offset of fuel delivery the unit has learned to hold the right mixture — LTFT is the steady fuel-delivery offset the unit has learned: how much it constantly has to add or cut fuel to hold stoichiometry. A large positive — lean, a large negative — rich.

When does it make sense to reset fuel trims?
  • Every day
  • Only after the cause is fixed (leak, injectors, pump, MAF), then a drive cycle
  • Instead of a repair
  • Never

Answer: Only after the cause is fixed (leak, injectors, pump, MAF), then a drive cycle — A reset lets the unit learn from a sound baseline — but only after the cause is repaired. Reset it with a live fault and the trim drifts back to ±25 % and the code returns.
