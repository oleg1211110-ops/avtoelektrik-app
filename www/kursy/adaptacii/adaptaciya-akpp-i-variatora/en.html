Automatic transmission and CVT adaptation

An automatic transmission and a CVT trim shift pressures and points to suit wear. After a repair, a fluid change or solenoid work, the old adaptations are reset so the transmission relearns from a sound baseline — otherwise jolts and kicks.

A modern automatic (and a CVT) is managed by a unit (TCU) that “learns” the clutch-fill pressures and shift points for the specific box and its wear. Those learned values are the transmission's adaptation.

When to reset

After a transmission repair, a fluid and filter change, valve-body or solenoid work, the old adaptations no longer match the new state. They're reset with a scan tool so the unit relearns; on some cars the adaptation happens on its own over a drive cycle per the make's procedure.

What happens after the reset

Right after the reset there may be light jolts and “hesitant” shifts — that's normal: the box relearns over the first few dozen kilometres along a set cycle (various speeds, gentle acceleration and braking). After the cycle the shifts become smooth.

Resetting adaptations doesn't fix a fault: if the jolts are from worn clutches, a clogged valve body, or low or old fluid, the adaptation won't remove them. Repair first and use the correct fluid to spec, then adapt. And check the transmission fluid level at the right temperature — a wrong level alone causes jolts.

Oleg's rule: jolts after a repair or a fluid change — first reset the adaptations and drive the cycle, and only if it doesn't clear, go deeper. “Permanent” jolts with no repair aren't fixed by a reset.
Key points
  • The TCU learns pressures and shift points to suit the box's wear; after a repair, a fluid change or solenoid work, the adaptations are reset.
  • Light jolts after the reset are normal — the box relearns over a drive cycle.
  • A reset doesn't fix wear, the valve body or bad fluid: repair first and fluid to spec, then adapt.

Check yourself

Why are there jolts after a transmission repair or a fluid change?
  • The transmission broke
  • The adaptations were reset or don't match — the box needs to relearn
  • Low on fuel
  • The spark plugs need replacing

Answer: The adaptations were reset or don't match — the box needs to relearn — The transmission unit learns pressures and shift points. After a repair or a fluid change the adaptations don't match the new state; after a reset the box relearns over a drive cycle and the jolts clear.

Will resetting adaptations remove jolts from worn clutches?
  • Yes, always
  • No — a reset doesn't fix wear; repair first with the correct fluid
  • Yes, if you reset it twice
  • Wear doesn't cause jolts

Answer: No — a reset doesn't fix wear; repair first with the correct fluid — Adaptation is a trim, not a repair. Worn clutches, a clogged valve body, old or wrong fluid aren't fixed by a reset: first remove the cause and fill fluid to spec, then adapt.
