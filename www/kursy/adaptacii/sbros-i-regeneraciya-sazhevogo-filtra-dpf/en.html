Resetting and regenerating the diesel particulate filter (DPF)

After cleaning or replacing the DPF, the stored soot and ash are reset in the unit — otherwise it thinks the filter is clogged. And when it's heavily loaded, a forced (service) regen is run with a scan tool, but only on a sound baseline.

The DPF traps soot, and the unit counts how much by the differential-pressure sensor and by a model (mileage, driving). After servicing, these counters must be put right, or the unit works on wrong data.

Resetting after replacement or cleaning

Fitted a new or cleaned filter — the unit's soot and ash counters (the stored “mass”) are reset, otherwise it treats the filter as clogged and endlessly asks for a regen or goes into limp mode. The reset is done with a scan tool, per the make's procedure.

Forced (service) regeneration

If the filter is loaded but still within limits, a scan tool runs a regen while parked: the unit raises the exhaust temperature and burns off the soot. It needs conditions — coolant temperature, fuel level, no active faults; the exhaust gets very hot, so it's done outdoors, away from people and anything flammable.

A forced regen must not be run on a heavily clogged filter (risk of overheating and cracking the ceramic) or with a high oil level (diluted with diesel) — change the oil first. And a reset doesn't fix the cause: if an over-fuelling injector or a leak is clogging the filter, the soot comes back.

The DPF is more often a victim than the cause. Before resetting and regenerating, find what's dirtying it (injectors, air, short trips). Gutting or deleting the filter is illegal (emissions), and we don't do it.
Key points
  • After replacing or cleaning a DPF, reset the soot and ash counters with a scan tool — otherwise the unit treats the filter as clogged.
  • A forced regen is run with a scan tool under the right conditions (temperature, fuel, no faults); not on a heavily clogged filter or with high oil.
  • A reset doesn't remove the cause: first find what's dirtying the filter.

Check yourself

Why reset the counters after replacing the DPF?
  • To add power
  • Otherwise the unit treats the new filter as clogged and asks for a regen or goes into limp mode
  • To erase the mileage
  • It isn't needed

Answer: Otherwise the unit treats the new filter as clogged and asks for a regen or goes into limp mode — The unit stores the accumulated soot and ash “mass.” Fit a new filter and the counters must be zeroed, or the unit runs on old data: endless regen requests or limp mode.

When must a forced regen NOT be run?
  • It's never allowed
  • On a heavily clogged filter and with a high oil level
  • On a warm engine
  • If the filter is clean

Answer: On a heavily clogged filter and with a high oil level — On a heavily clogged filter a regen risks overheating and cracking the ceramic, and with diluted oil (above MAX) you change the oil first. And you always remove the cause of the clogging first.
