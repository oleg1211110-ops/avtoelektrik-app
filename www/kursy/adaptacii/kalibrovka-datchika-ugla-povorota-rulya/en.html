Steering angle sensor calibration

After a wheel alignment, replacing the steering rack, tie rods, ends or removing the steering wheel, the steering angle sensor must be calibrated (zeroed). Otherwise ABS/ESP comes on.

The steering angle sensor tells the ESP/ABS unit which way the wheels are turned. Stability control compares that with the car’s yaw. If the steering “zero” is off, the system sees a mismatch and switches off with a code.

When calibration is needed

After a wheel alignment, replacing or removing the steering rack, tie rods and ends, the steering wheel or the sensor itself. Symptom: the ABS/ESP light (and often a steering icon) is on, and stability assistance doesn’t work.

How it's done

Calibration with a scan tool: wheels straight, run the “zero the steering angle sensor” procedure. On some cars — a drive cycle: drive straight and turn both ways per the procedure. After calibration the code clears.

Start from what was recently done to the steering or suspension: ABS/ESP on after an alignment or a rack replacement is almost always a steering-angle calibration, not a repair (ties in with the ABS-light topic).
Key points
  • The steering angle sensor is needed by ESP/ABS; a lost “zero” mismatches the system and disables stability control.
  • Calibration is needed after an alignment, a rack, tie rods, ends, removing the wheel or the sensor.
  • It’s done with a scan tool (wheels straight) or a drive cycle; first look at what was recently repaired.

Check yourself

ABS/ESP came on after a wheel alignment. Why?
  • ABS failed
  • The steering angle sensor zero is off — a calibration is needed
  • Out of brake fluid
  • A tire went flat

Answer: The steering angle sensor zero is off — a calibration is needed — During an alignment and steering work the steering angle sensor zero is knocked off, stability control sees a mismatch and switches off with a code. The sensor must be calibrated (zeroed) with a scan tool.

How is the steering angle sensor calibrated?
  • The whole ABS is replaced
  • Wheels straight and a scan-tool procedure (or a drive cycle with turns)
  • Disconnect the terminal for an hour
  • Bleed the brakes

Answer: Wheels straight and a scan-tool procedure (or a drive cycle with turns) — The wheels are set straight and the scan tool runs the steering-angle zeroing procedure; on some cars it’s a drive cycle with turns instead. After that the ABS/ESP code clears.
