Throttle body and idle relearn

After cleaning or replacing the throttle body, or disconnecting the battery, the unit loses the learned throttle position — idle hunts, runs high or stalls. A throttle relearn is needed.

An electronic throttle body holds the idle at the zero position the unit learned. After cleaning, replacing the throttle or disconnecting the battery that position is lost, and the idle hunts, sits high or the engine stalls.

How to adapt

On most cars — with a scan tool (the “throttle adaptation / reset idle adaptations” function). Some have a procedure without a scan tool: ignition on, wait, a few ignition cycles, or just drive it warm — the unit relearns on its own. The exact steps depend on the make.

Check first

Before the adaptation make sure there is no vacuum leak, no codes and no dirt in the bore — otherwise the hunting idle comes back. The adaptation doesn’t fix a leak; it only relearns the unit from a sound baseline (ties in with the rough-idle topic).

After cleaning the throttle body the adaptation is a must: otherwise you’ll chase a hunting or high idle, thinking the cleaning didn’t help.
Key points
  • An electronic throttle holds idle at the learned zero position; cleaning/replacing/disconnecting the terminal knocks it off.
  • The adaptation is done with a scan tool or an ignition procedure/drive cycle (depends on the make).
  • Clear the leak and codes first, then adapt — otherwise the hunting idle returns.
