AdBlue and SCR: what it's for and how to check it

What AdBlue and the SCR system do, what they consist of, why it crystallizes and goes into a power countdown — and what you can check yourself.

SCR reduces nitrogen oxides (NOx) in diesel exhaust. AdBlue — a urea solution (32.5% urea in water) — is injected into the hot exhaust: it breaks down into ammonia, and on the SCR catalyst NOx turns into nitrogen and water. A separate tank, pump, dosing injector and NOx sensors before and after. Without a working SCR a modern diesel goes into power limiting by law.

What it is made of

An AdBlue tank with level, quality and temperature sensors and a heater (AdBlue freezes at about −11 °C and is heated in winter). A pump and lines. The dosing unit (AdBlue injector) sprays the solution into the exhaust. NOx sensors before and after the SCR catalyst (sometimes an ammonia sensor) — the ECU uses them to see whether the cleanup is working.

Why it goes to a fault and countdown

Common causes: low level or poor fluid quality (water or the wrong fluid added), urea crystallization — a white deposit clogs the dosing unit and lines, a faulty dosing unit, the NOx or quality sensors, the tank heater in winter. By regulation, with a faulty or empty SCR the ECU starts a countdown: power is limited, and after a certain number of starts the engine may not start until the cause is fixed.

What you can do and how to check

Keep the tank topped up with quality AdBlue and don't let the fluid freeze. A white deposit (crystals) on the dosing unit and tubes — soak and clean it. In the scan tool check: fluid level and quality, the AdBlue dosing amount, NOx before and after the SCR (after should be noticeably lower), temperatures and the countdown. If the light is on, don't delay: the sooner the cheaper, before the no-start countdown begins.

From practice: before replacing an expensive dosing unit or NOx sensor, check the simple things — fluid quality and level and crystallization. Often they're to blame, not the electronics.
Key points
  • SCR cleans NOx: AdBlue is injected into the exhaust, and on the catalyst NOx → nitrogen and water.
  • Weak points: fluid level/quality, crystallization, the dosing unit, NOx sensors, the tank heater in winter.
  • A faulty or empty SCR means a countdown: power limiting and a no-start risk; check level, quality and NOx before/after in the scan tool.

Check yourself

What does the SCR system inject into the exhaust and why?
  • Oil, for lubrication
  • AdBlue, to reduce nitrogen oxides NOx
  • Petrol, for power
  • Water, for cooling

Answer: AdBlue, to reduce nitrogen oxides NOx — SCR injects AdBlue (32.5% in water) into the hot exhaust. It gives off ammonia, and on the SCR catalyst nitrogen oxides NOx turn into nitrogen and water.

There's a white deposit on the AdBlue dosing unit and tubes, the system has a fault. What is it?
  • Это нормально
  • Urea crystallization — it clogs the dosing unit and lines
  • An oil leak
  • Scale from antifreeze

Answer: Urea crystallization — it clogs the dosing unit and lines — The white deposit is urea crystals. They clog the dosing unit and lines, dosing is disrupted and the system faults. The crystals are soaked off and cleaned.

The AdBlue light is on and power has started to drop. Why can't you delay?
  • Nothing will happen
  • By regulation a countdown starts: power is limited, then the engine may not start until it's fixed
  • The battery will burn out
  • It's a gearbox failure

Answer: By regulation a countdown starts: power is limited, then the engine may not start until it's fixed — With a faulty or empty SCR a countdown runs by regulation: first power limiting, then after a number of starts the engine may not start until the cause is fixed.
