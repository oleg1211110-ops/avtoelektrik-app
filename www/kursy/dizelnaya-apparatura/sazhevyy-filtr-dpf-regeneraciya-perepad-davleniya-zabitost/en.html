Diesel particulate filter (DPF): regeneration, pressure difference, clogging

What a particulate filter is, how it cleans itself by regeneration, why it clogs from short trips, and how to check it without ruining the filter.

The particulate filter (DPF, on diesels) traps soot from the exhaust. To avoid clogging it periodically burns it off — that's regeneration. The ECU works out how much soot is in the filter from the differential pressure sensor (before and after the filter) and the mileage. Understanding regeneration explains half of DPF 'problems' at once.

Regeneration: passive and active

Passive — on the highway the exhaust is hot enough and soot burns off on its own. Active — the ECU raises exhaust temperature (late or extra injection) to burn soot when a lot has built up. During active regeneration idle revs are higher, fuel use rises a little and there may be a smell from the tailpipe — this is normal, no need to cut the trip short.

Why it clogs

The main cause is short city trips: the filter doesn't warm up, regeneration doesn't finish, soot builds up. Then — the DPF light, power loss, limp mode (derate). Faults also block regeneration: an air leak, bad injectors, the EGR valve, the differential pressure sensor and its tubes (clog with soot and read wrong).

What you can do and how to check

If the DPF light comes on, let the car drive on the highway for 20–30 minutes at above-average revs: that's often enough for regeneration. If it doesn't help — a forced regeneration with a scanner (watch temperature and conditions). In the scan tool check: soot load (%), pressure difference, temperatures before/after the filter, whether regeneration completes. Ash (the non-combustible residue) still builds up over the filter's life — then the filter is cleaned or replaced.

Before blaming the filter itself — check the differential pressure sensor and its tubes: soot-clogged tubes give a false 'filter clogged'. It's cheap and often solves it.
Key points
  • The DPF traps soot and burns it off by regeneration (passive on the highway and active from the ECU).
  • It mainly clogs from short trips; soot load, pressure difference and temperatures are checked in the scan tool.
  • First check the differential pressure sensor and its tubes — a common false 'filter clogged'.

Check yourself

The DPF light came on and the car only drives in the city. What's sensible to do first?
  • Immediately cut out the filter
  • Drive on the highway for 20–30 minutes at higher revs — let regeneration finish
  • Pour an additive in the tank
  • Replace the spark plugs

Answer: Drive on the highway for 20–30 minutes at higher revs — let regeneration finish — Short trips don't let the filter warm up and finish regeneration. Driving 20–30 minutes on the highway often helps; if not — a forced regeneration with a scanner and checking the causes (differential sensor, injectors, EGR).
