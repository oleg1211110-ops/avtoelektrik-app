EGR: exhaust recirculation and its carbon

Why a diesel needs an EGR valve, why it cokes up, how it behaves stuck open and stuck closed — and what you can do yourself (clean it, don't block it).

EGR (exhaust gas recirculation) routes some of the exhaust back into the intake. That lowers combustion temperature and nitrogen-oxide (NOx) output. On a diesel, large volumes pass through EGR, often via a cooler (EGR cooler), so the intake slowly clogs with an oily, sooty carbon.

How it is built

The EGR valve opens and closes the passage from exhaust to intake on the ECU's command; it can be vacuum or electric, often with a position sensor. Diesels add an EGR cooler — the gas passes through it and cools before the intake. The ECU watches actual flow and compares it with the target.

What goes wrong

The main ailment is carbon: the valve and intake manifold clog and the valve sticks. Stuck open — extra exhaust in the intake at idle, the engine shakes, idle is unstable, it may stall, with black smoke and a bog under load. Stuck closed — NOx rises, with a tendency to knock and overheat. The EGR cooler can also leak (coolant into the exhaust — white smoke, dropping coolant). Codes: P0401 flow too low, P0402 too high, P0404 range/performance, P0403 control circuit.

What you can do yourself

Read the codes and watch the actual EGR flow in the scanner. Remove the valve and clean it and the intake of carbon — that is often enough (the valve, like the VVT valve, clogs with dirt). Check the valve's connector and wiring and the position sensor. Don't block the EGR with a plate or a tune: it is illegal for emissions and brings other problems (codes, rising temperature, soot in other parts). First recall what was recently done to the intake.

The EGR valve and intake can often be removed and washed of carbon — cheaper than replacing. But if carbon has fully clogged the intake manifold, cleaning the valve won't help — look at the manifold too.
Key points
  • EGR routes some exhaust into the intake: lower combustion temperature and NOx; on a diesel, via a cooler, and the intake clogs with carbon.
  • Stuck open — shakes, stalls, smoke; stuck closed — knock, overheating, NOx; codes P0401/P0402/P0404/P0403.
  • Yourself: read the flow, remove and clean the valve and intake, check the connector; don't block it — illegal, and it brings other problems.

Check yourself

Why is the EGR valve needed?
  • To increase power
  • To route some exhaust into the intake to lower combustion temperature and NOx
  • To clean the oil
  • To charge the battery

Answer: To route some exhaust into the intake to lower combustion temperature and NOx — EGR routes some exhaust gas into the intake. That lowers combustion temperature and nitrogen-oxide (NOx) output. The price is carbon in the intake, especially on a diesel with an EGR cooler.

A diesel shakes at idle, black smoke, code P0401, carbon in the intake. Where do you start?
  • Replace the turbo
  • Remove and clean the EGR valve and intake of carbon, check the flow in the scanner
  • Fit an EGR blanking plate
  • Replace the injectors

Answer: Remove and clean the EGR valve and intake of carbon, check the flow in the scanner — Classic coked-up EGR. First remove and clean the valve and intake of carbon and watch the actual flow in the scanner. Blocking it with a plate is not allowed — illegal, and it brings other problems.
