Glow plugs and cold starting a diesel

Why a diesel needs glow plugs, how they work, why cold weather brings hard starting and white smoke — and what you can check yourself.

A diesel ignites from compression, but on a cold engine there isn't enough heated air to self-ignite. Glow plugs help — they warm the chamber before starting. Some diesels and trucks have an intake air heater (grid) instead of, or along with, them. The coil icon on the dash is exactly this preheating.

How it works

Turn on the ignition — the glow plugs heat for a few seconds (the coil icon is lit); once it goes out you can crank. After starting, many systems keep heating the plugs (afterglow) for smooth running and less smoke when cold. A relay or glow module controls it.

Signs of a fault

Hard start when cold, misfire for the first seconds, white smoke until the engine warms up. The glow icon is lit or a code appears. Most often the glow plugs themselves are to blame (one or several dead), less often the relay/glow module, wiring or fuse. In summer a diesel may start even with dead plugs — which is why it's noticed in winter.

What you can check

Preheat twice before starting (ignition on–off), especially in the cold. Check the fuse and the glow relay. Test the plugs by resistance (a good one has low resistance) or by current on each; on many modules the scanner shows which plug is open-circuit. Replace all the plugs at once — the rest are near the end too.

Don't force seized glow plugs out of a hot engine — they often snap off in the head. Better cold, with penetrating oil and gently; a broken-off plug is a separate big job.
Key points
  • Glow plugs warm the chamber for a cold start; there's also afterglow after starting.
  • Symptoms: hard cold start, misfire and white smoke, the glow icon; most often the plugs themselves are at fault.
  • Check: fuse and relay, plugs by resistance/current; replace all at once, unscrew carefully.

Check yourself

Why does a diesel need glow plugs?
  • To ignite the mixture with a spark, like gasoline
  • To warm the combustion chamber for a cold start
  • To charge the battery
  • To clean the exhaust

Answer: To warm the combustion chamber for a cold start — A diesel ignites from compression, but when cold the air isn't enough to heat up. Glow plugs warm the chamber before starting (and often keep heating after — afterglow) so the engine starts smoothly and smokes less.

In winter a diesel starts hard and smokes white for the first seconds, in summer it's fine. Where do you start?
  • Change the oil
  • Check the glow plugs, their relay and fuse
  • Replace the catalytic converter
  • Check the brakes

Answer: Check the glow plugs, their relay and fuse — Classic dead glow plugs: in summer it starts even without them, but when cold — hard start, misfire and white smoke. You check the plugs (by resistance/current), the relay and the glow fuse.
