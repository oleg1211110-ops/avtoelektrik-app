Diesel fuel: water, air and the separator

Two cheap killers of diesel fuel equipment — water and air in the fuel. Why the separator matters, how to catch air in the fuel and prime the system, and what to do with dirty or wrong fuel.

A diesel's pump and injectors are lubricated by the fuel itself and run at huge pressure, so they are very demanding about fuel. The two most common enemies — cheap in cause, expensive in consequence — are water and air. Let's see how to keep them out of the equipment and how to find them yourself.

Water in the fuel and the separator

Water gets in with bad fuel and from condensation in the tank. A water separator is fitted for it — often the lower part of the fuel filter, with a bowl and a sensor. Water is heavier than diesel and collects at the bottom. If it isn't drained, it goes into the pump and injectors: it washes away lubrication and causes corrosion and scoring — one of the main reasons for expensive fuel-equipment repairs. If the 'water in fuel' light is on, drain the bowl; drain it on schedule anyway.

Air in the fuel (air leak)

On the suction line (from the tank to the pump) the pressure is below atmospheric, so air is drawn in through any leak while fuel does not drip out — which is why a suction leak isn't always visible. Signs: hard starting, the engine starts then stalls, a hunting idle, bubbles in a clear filter housing or bowl. A common case is air in the fuel after a filter change. You look for the leak on the suction side: the filter housing and lid, fittings, seals, the hand primer pump.

What you can do yourself

Drain the separator bowl (check for water and dirt). Prime the system with the hand primer until firm and bleed the air out. Check the suction line for a leak and change the filter on schedule. Recall what was put in: gasoline in a diesel (misfuel) — don't start or drive it, drain it; cloudy or dirty fuel clogs the filter (ties in with 'diesel won't start'). While cranking, there should be return flow to the tank — if there isn't, fuel isn't getting through.

Before you get into the pump and injectors, check the cheap things first: drain the separator bowl and find the air leak on the suction side. Water and air kill the equipment quietly, but they are found fast and for free.
Key points
  • The pump and injectors are lubricated by fuel and fear water and air; these are troubles cheap in cause but expensive in consequence.
  • The separator (the filter bowl) catches water — drain it by the light/schedule, or you get corrosion and scoring in the equipment.
  • Air in the fuel: a leak on the suction side (fuel doesn't drip out); bubbles in the bowl, start-then-stall — find the leak and prime it.
