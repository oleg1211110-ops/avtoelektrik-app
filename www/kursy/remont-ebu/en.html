ECU repair: a course for auto electricians

For those who have completed the auto electrician course and want to repair the control modules themselves: how an ECU is built, the workbench, connecting on the bench, troubleshooting, soldering, module memory and handing the unit back to the customer.

What you’ll learn

  • Understand an ECU board: the 5 V supply, processor, memory, inputs and outputs
  • Power a unit on the bench and build a permanent test bench — even without a wiring diagram
  • Find the cause when a unit won’t communicate
  • Test output stages and board components and choose replacements
  • Solder SMD parts and restore a board after moisture and corrosion
  • Back up the memory before any repair and test the unit before handing it back

Syllabus

18 lessons · about 163 minutes · The pictures in the lessons are animated, and every lesson has a hands-on trainer or lab, so you can try what it describes yourself.

  1. 1

    What’s inside an ECU

    What sections make up a control module’s circuit board — the power supply and 5 V regulator, the processor and crystal, memory, input circuits, output stages, bus transceivers — and how they connect to the connector.

    8 min · 4 questionsfree

    Workbench and safety

    What you need to repair modules on the bench — antistatic protection, a bench power supply with current limiting, a soldering station, a hot-air station, magnification and cleaning — what you must never do, and how to remove and install a module on the car.

    8 min · 4 questionsfree
  3. 3

    Connecting a module on the bench

    How to power a module on the bench: +30, +15, all grounds, the main relay and output power, the diagnostic line to the adapter, 12 and 24 V — and how to tell from the current whether the module has woken up.

    9 min · 4 questionsfree
  4. 4

    A DIY test bench for modules

    What a permanent module test bench is built from — a power supply with current limiting, a fuse, “+30” and “+15” switches, a main relay, adapter harnesses and a jack panel, loads, a diagnostic line, a crankshaft simulator and an oscilloscope — and how to check the bench before the first module.

    9 min · 4 questionsfree
  5. 5

    A module that won’t communicate

    The order of checks when the scan tool can’t see a module: power and grounds at the connector, current draw, the 5 V regulator, the crystal, the CAN transceiver, traces and vias, signs of moisture — and how to tell a module fault from a fault in the car’s wiring.

    9 min · 4 questionsfree
  6. 6

    Output stages: why they burn out and how to test them

    Why injector, coil, relay and motor drivers burn out, how to test a module output with a load simulator and an oscilloscope, and why the cause in the car’s wiring has to be fixed first.

    9 min · 4 questionsfull course
