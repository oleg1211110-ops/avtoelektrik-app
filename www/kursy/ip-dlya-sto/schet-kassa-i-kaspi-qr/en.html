Bank account, register, and Kaspi QR

How to take payment for repairs in 2026: the register and fiscal receipt for cash, card, and QR; Kaspi Kassa; transfers from companies to the IP account; the 1,000 MCI rule; and why payment to a personal card is a risk.

Customers pay in different ways: cash, card, Kaspi QR, and fleets by transfer to your account. Each method has its own paperwork. This lesson covers what to give the customer, where to take the money, and where people most often slip up. The key point: all money for repairs is IP income, however the customer pays.

The register and the fiscal receipt

According to clarifications in open sources (mybuh.kz, 2025–2026), payments in cash, by card, by QR code (Kaspi Pay and others), and remote payments are cash settlements. They go through a cash register (KKM), and the customer gets a fiscal receipt. The code does exempt some from the KKM, per open sources, for example lawyers, mediators, and private bailiffs, but auto repair shops aren’t among them. Confirm the article number in the new code and the full list of exemptions with the KGD.

  • Cash: a fiscal KKM receipt for the full amount paid.
  • Card through a terminal: a KKM receipt with payment type “card.”
  • Kaspi QR and remote payment: a KKM receipt with payment type “mobile.”
  • A transfer from a company to the IP account by invoice usually needs no KKM receipt, per open sources. The paperwork here is different, an act and an ESF; they’re covered in the lesson on fleets.

A common mistake is QR with no receipt. A Kaspi bank receipt is a transfer confirmation, not a fiscal receipt. Per open sources, a QR payment, even to the IP account, doesn’t remove the need for a fiscal receipt: ring it up on the KKM with payment type “mobile.”

Kaspi Kassa

According to tax officials at a seminar of the Atameken National Chamber of Entrepreneurs (as reported by finratings.kz, date not given): if the Kaspi Kassa feature is turned on in Kaspi Pay, the payment is fiscalized automatically and the customer gets an electronic fiscal receipt, so no separate KKM receipt is needed; without this feature you need a separate online KKM. No official document confirms this, so check with the KGD and your bank that your register actually issues a fiscal receipt.

The IP account and the 1,000 MCI rule

In practice the IP bank account is opened right at registration through a bank, for example Halyk “Stat IP” or Kaspi Pay. Fleets and companies pay into it, and your QR code is linked to it. When all money for work comes into the IP account and goes through the register, it’s easy to reconcile with Form 910.00.

The 1,000 MCI rule. Under Art. 25(9) of the Law “On Payments and Payment Systems” (per mybuh.kz, 2023), settlements over 1,000 MCI in a single deal (over ₸4,325,000 in 2026) must be non-cash between legal entities, between a legal entity and a VAT-registered IP, and between VAT-registered IPs. An IP on the simplified regime doesn’t pay VAT, so, per the same source, its settlements with LLPs and individuals have no such limit. Check with your bank before a large deal.

A personal card is a risk

Payment for repairs to the mechanic’s personal card isn’t a saving, it’s a risk. It’s still IP income: you must report it on Form 910.00 and give the customer a receipt. According to accounting websites (mybuh.kz, May 24, 2026), since 2026 banks report transfers to individuals’ cards to the KGD when certain criteria are met. If that money isn’t on the return, you face additional tax, late-payment interest, and a fine.

One more rule, from the Law “On Consumer Protection” (Art. 8-1): the price when paying by card can’t be higher than when paying cash. One price for any payment method.

Key points
  • Cash, card, and Kaspi QR are cash settlements: per open sources, they need a KKM and a fiscal receipt.
  • A Kaspi bank receipt isn’t fiscal. Per a clarification at an Atameken NCE seminar, Kaspi Kassa fiscalizes the payment itself; confirm with the KGD.
  • A transfer from a company to the IP account: an act and an ESF. The 1,000 MCI rule (₸4,325,000 in 2026) applies to legal entities and VAT payers.
  • Payment to a personal card is a risk: take money to the IP account and with a receipt; the card price is no higher than the cash price.

Check yourself

A driver paid for a repair by Kaspi QR and got a bank receipt. Is that enough?
  • Yes, a bank receipt is fiscal too
  • No, a fiscal KKM receipt with payment type “mobile” is needed
  • Yes, if the amount is under 1,000 MCI

Answer: No, a fiscal KKM receipt with payment type “mobile” is needed — According to clarifications in open sources, a QR payment is a cash settlement with a fiscal receipt. A Kaspi bank receipt isn’t fiscal.

A customer offers to send payment for a repair to the mechanic’s personal card. What is that?
  • A risk: it’s IP income bypassing the register and the books, and banks report such transfers to the KGD
  • A legal way to avoid tax
  • Fine if the amount is small

Answer: A risk: it’s IP income bypassing the register and the books, and banks report such transfers to the KGD — Money for repairs is IP income on any card. If it’s not on the return, you face additional tax, late-payment interest, and a fine.

An LLP fleet pays the IP account by invoice. What paperwork is needed?
  • Only a KKM receipt
  • Nothing: the money is already in the account
  • A certificate of completed work (act) and an ESF

Answer: A certificate of completed work (act) and an ESF — Per open sources, a transfer from a company account by invoice usually goes without a KKM receipt. The act closes out the work, and since 2026 simplified-regime businesses issue ESFs.
