Parts: buying, selling, tracking

Whose part goes into the car, yours or the customer’s; when selling parts becomes trade; which documents to get from a supplier; why the whole part price counts as income; what the Virtual Warehouse is; and how to track stock.

Lesson 10 of 15 · 9 min · “IP for a repair shop” course · auto electrician Oleg, Almaty, 26 years’ experience

A part at a repair shop comes one of two ways: the customer brings their own, or you install your own, bought from a supplier. For paperwork and taxes these are different cases. In the first you sell only labor. In the second you also sell goods: that’s trade, with its own OKED code, documents, and records.

Your part or the customer’s

  • The customer brought their own part: you sell a service, so the work order, completion certificate, and receipt show labor only.
  • You install your own part: you sell both labor and goods. On the work order and receipt these are separate lines.
  • If you sell parts, you need an OKED code from group 45.32 (retail sale of parts), as in the OKED lesson. Section 45 is allowed on the simplified regime: it isn’t on list No. 970.
  • Note on the work order whose part it is: then the papers show what you sold, just labor or labor and goods.

Documents from the supplier

  • A waybill for every purchase: what arrived, how many, and at what price. It’s a source document, and you keep it.
  • ESF: per the KGD, since 2026 e-invoices must be issued by simplified-regime businesses too, not only VAT payers (Art. 208(1) of the Tax Code). If you pay by bank transfer, find the supplier’s ESF in the ESF system and check it against the waybill.
  • Bought at a store with a fiscal receipt? A sale with a cash register (KKM) receipt needs no ESF (Art. 208(2) of the Tax Code). Keep the receipt; you can ask for your IIN to be printed on it.
  • A part without a document can’t be backed up at an inventory count: get a waybill or receipt for every purchase.

Tracking stock

On the simplified regime you may skip accounting books while income stays at or under 135,000 MCI a year (₸583,875,000 in 2026) and you aren’t a VAT payer. But preparing and keeping source documents, keeping tax records, and taking inventory are always required (Art. 202(4) of the Tax Code, KGD clarification). For parts a simple sheet is enough: received per the waybill, used per the work order and receipt, and stock as the difference. Count the shelf now and then and check it against the sheet.

Money for a part is income too

On the simplified regime tax is figured on income with no deduction for expenses (Article 724 of the Tax Code, per a copy of the code in public sources). Income is everything the customer paid, for labor and for the part. The price you paid the supplier for the part isn’t deducted. Income is adjusted for refunds, discounts, and price changes.

Example: you bought a sensor for ₸20,000, installed it for the customer at ₸24,000, and charged ₸10,000 for labor. Income for Form 910.00 is ₸34,000, not ₸14,000 of “earnings.” Tax is on the part’s full price, not the markup, so factor that in when you set prices. Ring up the part through the register together with the labor: one receipt for the full amount the customer paid (more on the register in the lesson on accounts, registers, and Kaspi QR).

The Virtual Warehouse

The Virtual Warehouse is part of the ESF system that tracks the movement of goods on a separate Ministry of Finance list. Since January 1, 2026 the list has been updated by an order of October 31, 2025 (sources give its number differently: No. 661 or No. 681). Per public sources (pro1c.kz), it added petroleum products of group 2710 (gasolines, fuels, fuel oil, and others), plus bitumen, propane and butane, and acids. Tires, batteries, and auto parts aren’t among the added items; whether they’re on the list at all, check by the product code or ask the KGD. Per a KGD answer (Art. 208(3) of the Tax Code), Virtual Warehouse goods and imported goods can be sold without an ESF only to an individual for personal use or to a micro-business; any other business needs an ESF.

Key points
  • Customer’s part: you sell only labor; your own part: goods too, with a group 45.32 code and a separate line on the work order and receipt.
  • A waybill or receipt for every purchase; check the supplier’s ESF against the waybill. Stock: in, out, on hand, plus regular inventory counts.
  • On the simplified regime income is the full amount the customer paid, for labor and parts alike; buying from a supplier doesn’t reduce it.
  • Virtual Warehouse and imported goods: per a KGD answer, no ESF only for an individual buying for personal use or a micro-business.

Check yourself

The customer brought their own part and you installed it. What did you sell?
  • Labor and the part
  • Labor only
  • Only the part

Answer: Labor only — The customer’s part is their goods. You sell a service: the work order, completion certificate, and receipt show labor only.

You bought a sensor for ₸20,000, installed it for the customer at ₸24,000, and charged ₸10,000 for labor. What’s the income for Form 910.00?
  • 14 000 ₸
  • ₸4,000
  • ₸34,000

Answer: ₸34,000 — On the simplified regime income has no expense deductions: 10,000 + 24,000 = ₸34,000. Buying from a supplier doesn’t reduce it.

What must a simplified-regime IP always do, even when allowed to skip accounting books?
  • Prepare and keep source documents and take inventory
  • File financial statements
  • Keep full accounting books

Answer: Prepare and keep source documents and take inventory — Under Art. 202(4) of the Tax Code and the KGD clarification, source documents are prepared and kept, and tax records and inventory counts are kept up, always.
