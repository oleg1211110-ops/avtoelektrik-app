Fleets and companies

How to work with fleets, companies, and IPs: contract, certificate of completed work (act), ESF (required for simplified-regime businesses too since 2026), reconciliation statement; when to issue an ESF and when a receipt; and why your regime matters to a client on the general regime.

A fleet, a taxi company, a logistics firm: clients that bring steady work. They usually pay not in cash but by transfer to the IP account, and there’s more paperwork: a contract, an act for each job, an ESF, a reconciliation statement. Let’s go through it in order.

Contract

With a regular client you sign a service contract. It helps to include:

  • which work and at what prices, with a price list attached;
  • how requests are made: a work order for each vehicle;
  • who on the client’s side accepts the work and signs the acts;
  • payment terms and the IP account details;
  • ESF and reconciliation: how often you reconcile.

Act and ESF

Each completed job is closed with a certificate of completed work (act), a source document. Per the KGD, for a buyer on the general regime, the date the service is received is the date the act is signed. So sign the act right after the work, not in a batch at the end of the month.

Since 2026, simplified-regime businesses, though they don’t pay VAT, must issue electronic invoices, ESFs (Art. 208(1) of the Tax Code, per the KGD clarification of Jan. 27, 2026). No ESF is needed if the sale went through with a KKM receipt, a terminal, or a special app, or when selling to an individual paying by card or e-money (Art. 208(2)). The “ESF for deals over 1,000 MCI” rule was removed from the code. Confirm the ESF deadline for simplified-regime businesses with the KGD, and better yet don’t wait: issue it as soon as the act is signed.

Reconciliation

Once a quarter or on request, reconcile with the fleet: how much work under acts, how much paid, what’s owed. Both sides sign the reconciliation statement. A discrepancy is a reason to look for an act that wasn’t signed or got lost, or a payment recorded differently. All receipts from companies are IP income for Form 910.00 (details in the “IP bookkeeping” course).

A client on the general regime and your regime

If a fleet is on the general regime, keep in mind: a buyer on the general regime can’t deduct expenses bought from a simplified-regime business (Art. 286(16) of the Tax Code, per the KGD). So such a client may ask about your regime before signing or choose a contractor on the general regime. Whether to change regimes is a decision to make with the numbers; it’s covered in the lesson on changing regimes.

Money from a fleet goes only to the IP account, under an act and with an ESF. A request like “we’ll send it to the driver’s card” or “we’ll pay without paperwork” is a risk for both: your income bypasses the books, and the client has no documents for the expense.

Key points
  • With a fleet: a contract, a work order and act for each job, payment to the IP account, an ESF, a reconciliation statement.
  • Since 2026, simplified-regime businesses issue ESFs; none is needed for a sale with a KKM receipt or to an individual paying by card.
  • For a buyer on the general regime, the service date is the date the act is signed: sign acts right away.
  • A buyer on the general regime can’t deduct expenses bought from a simplified-regime business; keep this in mind before signing.

Check yourself

A fleet paid for a repair under an act by transfer to the IP account, with no receipt rung up. Is an ESF needed?
  • No, simplified-regime businesses don’t issue ESFs
  • Yes, since 2026 simplified-regime businesses must issue ESFs
  • Only if the amount exceeds 1,000 MCI

Answer: Yes, since 2026 simplified-regime businesses must issue ESFs — Art. 208(1) of the Tax Code: since 2026, simplified-regime businesses issue ESFs. The 1,000 MCI rule for ESFs was removed.

For a buyer on the general regime, which date counts as the date the service was received?
  • The date the act is signed
  • The payment date
  • The request date

Answer: The date the act is signed — Per the KGD, it’s the date the certificate of completed work (act) is signed. Sign acts right after the work.

Why does your tax regime matter to a fleet on the general regime?
  • On the simplified regime you must take prepayment
  • A simplified-regime business can’t issue an act
  • It can’t deduct expenses bought from a simplified-regime business

Answer: It can’t deduct expenses bought from a simplified-regime business — Art. 286(16) of the Tax Code: a buyer on the general regime can’t deduct expenses bought from a simplified-regime business.
