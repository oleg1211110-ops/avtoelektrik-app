Safety: high voltage, airbags, carbon monoxide

Where a car’s electrics and exhaust are genuinely dangerous: hybrids and EVs, airbags, xenon lights, carbon monoxide and working under the hood.

The regular 12-volt system won’t give you an electric shock. But a car has parts where a mistake can cost you your health or your life. Every driver should know about them.

Hybrids and electric vehicles

Besides the regular 12-volt system, they have a high-voltage battery of hundreds of volts — and that can kill. By common convention, high-voltage cables and connectors are orange.

  • Don’t touch, disconnect or cut orange cables and connectors — only a certified specialist works on them.
  • High voltage stays in the battery even after the car is switched off.
  • In READY mode the car can move off silently. Before reaching under the hood or near the wheels, switch it off and take the key away.
  • After a crash, don’t touch a damaged battery: it may leak, heat up or catch fire. Move away and call the emergency services.
  • Jump-start a hybrid, or from a hybrid, only via the points given in the manual.

Airbags

  • Don’t measure the resistance of airbag squibs (igniters) with a multimeter: the meter’s current can set them off. The system’s connectors are usually yellow.
  • Before working near the airbags — steering wheel, dashboard, seats — switch off the ignition, disconnect the battery and wait as long as the manual specifies: the control unit stores a reserve of energy.
  • Carry a removed airbag module with the cover facing away from you, and set it down cover up.
  • Don’t put a rear-facing child seat in the front seat with the airbag active.
  • If the airbag light is on, the system may not work in a crash — don’t put off the diagnostics.

Xenon

A xenon headlight’s ballast puts out a pulse of tens of thousands of volts the moment it’s switched on. Touch the bulb and the ballast only with the lights off and the connector unplugged. Don’t touch the glass of a halogen bulb with your fingers: the greasy mark overheats the glass and the bulb burns out quickly.

Carbon monoxide

Carbon monoxide has no color or smell and can kill you in your sleep. Never leave the engine or a parking heater running in a closed garage. If the car is snowed in, clear the exhaust pipe first, and only then warm up with the engine running. The parking heater’s exhaust must vent outside. Headache, drowsiness, nausea — get to fresh air immediately.

Under the hood

  • Don’t disconnect the battery with the engine running: a voltage spike can damage the electronics.
  • Disconnect the negative first and reconnect it last.
  • Take off rings, watches and bracelets: metal that shorts the positive to the body instantly gets red-hot and causes a severe burn.
  • The radiator fan can switch on by itself even with the engine off — keep your hands clear.
  • A battery means acid and hydrogen: goggles, gloves, no sparks or flames. If acid gets in your eyes, rinse with plenty of water for at least 15 minutes and see a doctor.
  • Before touching control unit connectors, touch bare metal on the body to discharge static; don’t touch the pins with your fingers.
  • Never get under a car held up only by a jack — only on solid jack stands.

Key points
  • Orange cables mean high voltage; only a certified specialist works on them.
  • Airbag squibs are never measured; before working, disconnect the battery and wait as long as the manual specifies.
  • Xenon puts out tens of thousands of volts when switched on — work on it only with the lights off.
  • Carbon monoxide can’t be seen or smelled: never run the engine or a parking heater in a closed garage.
  • Don’t disconnect the battery with the engine running, take off rings and watches, and discharge static by touching the body.

Check yourself

What color are the high-voltage cables in hybrids and electric vehicles?
  • Orange
  • Yellow
  • Red

Answer: Orange — Orange is the standard marking for high voltage. Yellow is usually used for airbag connectors.

Why must you not measure the resistance of an airbag squib with a multimeter?
  • The multimeter will break
  • The radio settings will reset
  • The meter’s current can deploy the airbag

Answer: The meter’s current can deploy the airbag — In resistance mode the multimeter passes current through the squib, and that can be enough to set it off.

Is it OK to warm up the car with the engine running in a closed garage?
  • Yes, if it’s not for long
  • No: colorless, odorless carbon monoxide can kill
  • Yes, if you leave a car window slightly open

Answer: No: colorless, odorless carbon monoxide can kill — In an enclosed space, carbon monoxide quickly builds up to a dangerous concentration, and there’s no way to notice it.
