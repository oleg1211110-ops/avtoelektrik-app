Dashboard warning lights

What red, yellow and green lights on the dash mean, and when you must not keep driving.

The color of a dashboard light tells you how serious things are. The system is the same for all cars and works like a traffic light.

  • Red — danger: stop as soon as it’s safe and find out what’s wrong.
  • Yellow (amber) — a fault or a warning: you can usually keep driving, but get it checked soon.
  • Green and blue — information: headlights, turn signal or cruise control on; blue means high beam.

When you switch on the ignition, most lights come on for a couple of seconds — that’s a self-check. They should go out after the engine starts. If a light doesn’t come on at all when you switch on the ignition, that’s also a reason to get it checked.

Red: when you must not drive

  • Oil pressure (oil can) — switch off the engine immediately. Without oil pressure the engine can be wrecked in a matter of minutes. Check the level; if it’s fine but the light is still on, the car can only go on a tow truck from there.
  • Coolant temperature (thermometer) — the engine is overheating. Pull over, switch off the engine and let it cool down.
  • Charging (battery symbol) — the alternator isn’t charging and the car is running on the battery’s reserve. Stop and check the belt: if it’s broken and it also drives the water pump, the engine will overheat quickly. If the belt is intact, you can make it to a repair shop with unnecessary loads switched off, but the charge won’t last long.
  • Brake system (exclamation mark in a circle) — if the parking brake is released, the brake fluid may be low. Don’t drive with faulty brakes.
  • Airbags (seated figure with a circle) — the airbags and seat belt pretensioners may not deploy in a crash. You can drive on, but don’t put off the diagnostics.

Don’t open the expansion tank or radiator cap on a hot engine: the coolant is under pressure and can spray out boiling hot.

Yellow: get it checked soon

  • Check Engine (engine outline) stays on — the control unit has stored a fault code. You can keep driving, but have the codes read soon.
  • Check Engine flashing — usually misfires. Unburned fuel can destroy the catalytic converter. Ease off and get to a shop as soon as you can; if the engine is shaking badly, stop.
  • ABS — the regular brakes still work, but the wheels can lock up. Brake more gently.
  • ESP (car with skid marks) — flashes when the car slides: the system is working, that’s normal. Stays on — the system is switched off or faulty.
  • Tire pressure — stop and check the tires.
  • Coil symbol on a diesel — the glow plugs are heating. Wait until it goes out and only then start the engine. If it flashes while driving, on many diesels that signals a fault.

Symbols vary slightly between makes; for the exact meaning, see the manual. For AdBlue lights, see the “AdBlue (urea)” section on the “Error codes” tab.

If several lights come on at once — ABS, ESP, airbags, Check Engine — there may be a single cause: low voltage, a bad ground or a communication fault between the units. Start by checking the battery.

Key points
  • Red — stop, yellow — get it checked soon, green and blue — information.
  • Oil pressure light — switch off the engine immediately.
  • Overheating — stop and let it cool; don’t open the coolant tank while it’s hot.
  • A flashing Check Engine is more dangerous than a steady one: the catalytic converter suffers.
  • Lots of lights at once — often one cause: voltage, ground or communication between units.

Check yourself

The red oil pressure light comes on while you’re driving. What should you do?
  • Drive home and look at it there
  • Stop immediately and switch off the engine
  • Rev the engine to raise the pressure

Answer: Stop immediately and switch off the engine — Without oil pressure the engine can be wrecked in minutes. Switch it off and check the level; if it’s fine, call a tow truck.

How is a flashing Check Engine different from one that stays on?
  • It isn’t — they’re the same thing
  • Flashing is just a service reminder
  • Flashing means the fault has already been cleared
  • Flashing usually means misfires that can destroy the catalytic converter

Answer: Flashing usually means misfires that can destroy the catalytic converter — Flashing is the more serious signal: unburned fuel overheats the catalytic converter. Ease off and get to a shop as soon as you can.

The ESP light flashes while the car is sliding on ice. Is that a fault?
  • No, that’s the stability control working
  • Yes, you need to get to a shop urgently
  • Yes, the ESP fuse has blown

Answer: No, that’s the stability control working — A flashing ESP light while sliding means the system has stepped in. It’s a fault when the light stays on.
