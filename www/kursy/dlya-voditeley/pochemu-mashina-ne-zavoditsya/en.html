Why the car won’t start

Step-by-step logic: starter, dashboard and immobilizer, fuel pump priming, fault codes — from simple to complex.

When the car won’t start, don’t guess or start replacing parts right away. Go from simple to complex: what happens when you turn the key already tells you where to look.

Step 1. Does the starter crank?

  • Silence, with the gauges dim or going out — a dead battery or a poor contact at the terminals. Check the voltage, how tight and clean the terminals are, and the ground. On some cars the engine computer protects a deeply discharged battery and won’t even send the start command — hence total silence.
  • The starter cranks slowly — a weak battery (especially in winter) or corroded terminals.
  • Rapid clicking (click-click-click) when you turn the key to Start or press Start, with the dash dimming badly, is also a dead battery or corroded terminals: check the terminals, jump-start the car and replace the battery if needed.
  • A single click, but the engine doesn’t turn even though the battery is charged — a starter fault: solenoid, brushes, contacts. If the dash also dims badly, the starter is drawing all the current.
  • Nothing at all, even though the battery is charged — check the interlocks: on an automatic, the selector must be in P or N; on a manual, the clutch must be pressed; on a push-button car, the brake must be pressed. Then check the starter fuse and relay.
If the starter gives a single click but doesn’t turn (with a charged battery), have a helper hold the key in Start and, at that moment, give the starter housing a few firm taps with a hammer. Tapping without the key in Start is useless.

If the starter then cranks, it’s worn and needs repairing before it fails completely. Hit the housing, not the wires or terminals; the car should be on the parking brake with the ignition off.

Step 2. It cranks but won’t start — look at the dashboard

A flashing key or car-with-a-lock symbol usually means the immobilizer hasn’t recognized the key. Typical signs: the engine doesn’t catch at all, or it starts for 1–2 seconds and stalls. Try the spare key and replace the key fob battery. On push-button cars with a dead fob battery, you hold the key against the start button or place it in a special spot — the manual tells you where. More in the “Immobilizer” section on the “Error codes” tab.

Step 3. Is the fuel pump priming?

Switch on the ignition without starting the engine and listen: on most cars the fuel pump will hum for 2–3 seconds — the sound comes from under the rear seat or from the tank. And don’t forget the obvious: is there fuel in the tank?

Check the fuses first. If the pump is silent, the first thing to look at is the fuel pump fuse and relay.
If the fuel pump isn’t working, tap lightly on the underside of the tank. If the pump “wakes up”, it’s worn and due for replacement.

On a diesel in winter, wait for the glow plug light to go out. In a hard frost, thickened summer diesel can also be the cause.

Step 4. Read the codes

The starter cranks, the key is recognized, the pump is working, but the engine won’t start — time to plug in a scan tool. The engine control unit often points to the cause: for example, there’s no signal from the crankshaft position sensor, and without it there’s neither spark nor injection. How to read codes is in the next lesson; sensor specs are in the “Sensors” section.

Don’t crank the starter for more than 10 seconds at a time; pause for 30–60 seconds — otherwise you’ll drain the battery and overheat the starter. Don’t check for spark by holding an ignition lead in your hand: the ignition puts out tens of thousands of volts. A car with an automatic transmission must not be push-started.

Key points
  • First see whether the starter cranks: silence, slow cranking or rapid clicks — battery and terminals; a single click with a charged battery — the starter.
  • Cranks but won’t start — look at the dash: a flashing key points to the immobilizer.
  • When you switch on the ignition, the pump should hum for 2–3 seconds; if it’s silent — fuse and relay.
  • Everything works but the engine won’t start — read the codes.
  • Starter — no more than 10 seconds at a time, with pauses.

Check yourself

The starter only clicks, the engine doesn’t turn, the battery is charged. Where is the fault most likely?
  • In the fuel pump
  • In the starter: the solenoid, brushes or contacts
  • In the immobilizer

Answer: In the starter: the solenoid, brushes or contacts — The immobilizer and the pump don’t affect whether the starter turns. A click with no cranking and a charged battery is a typical starter fault.

The starter cranks briskly, but the engine won’t start and a key symbol is flashing on the dash. What’s the first thing to do?
  • Replace the spark plugs
  • Jump-start from another car
  • Tap on the starter
  • Try the spare key — the immobilizer probably didn’t recognize the key

Answer: Try the spare key — the immobilizer probably didn’t recognize the key — A flashing key is an immobilizer signal. The starter is fine, and the spare key will quickly show whether the main one is to blame.

How can you check whether the fuel pump works without any tools?
  • Switch on the ignition and listen: the pump should hum for 2–3 seconds
  • Undo a fuel hose on a hot engine
  • Crank the starter for a minute without stopping

Answer: Switch on the ignition and listen: the pump should hum for 2–3 seconds — On most cars the pump briefly builds up pressure when you switch on the ignition, and you can hear it from the tank.
