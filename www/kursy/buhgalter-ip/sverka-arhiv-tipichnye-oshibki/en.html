Reconciliation, archive, typical mistakes

How to close a half-year with no surprises: a reconciliation statement with a fleet, register — bank — Form 910.00, checking for debt in your personal account, a document archive, and typical IP mistakes. At the end, a final test for the course.

This last lesson pulls the course together. Before every Form 910.00 — better yet, every month — do three checks: with business clients, register — bank — return, and your personal account. Then build the archive. The example is the shop from lesson 6: H2 2026, Form 910.00 due by Feb 15, 2027. At the end, a final test for the whole course.

Reconciling with a fleet — the bookkeeper’s view

In the “IP for a repair shop” course, the reconciliation statement was covered as a document with the client. For the books, it shows whether an act or a payment went missing. Keep a table for each fleet: the act (number, date, amount), its ESF, and the payment per the bank statement. Before Form 910.00, reconcile the whole half-year:

  • the opening balance — the same for you and the client;
  • acts — by number, date, and amount; for an act paid by transfer without a receipt, an ESF for the same amount (lesson 10);
  • payments — by date and amount from the bank statement, not from memory;
  • the closing balance and both sides’ signatures.

Example. In H2 2026 the fleet’s acts total ₸1,240,000. Your table shows ₸1,090,000 paid and ₸150,000 owed; the fleet’s records show nothing owed. The bank statement has a ₸150,000 payment of Dec 28, 2026 that never went into the table. Without reconciling, income on Form 910.00 would be ₸150,000 short, and you’d have to file an amended return (lesson 8).

Register — bank — Form 910.00, and your personal account

The second check is income, as in lesson 3, using the half-year’s documents: KKM receipts per register reports plus payments from companies per the bank statement minus refunds = income on Form 910.00. In the example: 5,860,000 + 1,240,000 − 40,000 = ₸7,060,000. If the bank shows more QR and card payments than receipts, look for a payment with no fiscal receipt.

The third check is your personal account (lesson 4): the balance for the tax and for payments for yourself and your employees should be 0. Pay any debt right away — interest grows every day (lesson 7). A debt on one code and an overpayment on another usually means a wrong KBK or KNP (lesson 5); ask your tax office (UGD) how to offset an overpayment.

The half-year archive

  • Monthly folders with source documents (lesson 9).
  • Form 910.00 with the tax payment receipt, Form 200.00 for each quarter.
  • Receipts for payments for yourself and your employees, bank statements.
  • Signed reconciliation statements, employment contracts, and payroll sheets.
  • Scans in the cloud as well: paper gets lost and fades.

We couldn’t confirm the retention period for source documents in official sources — check with the KGD. A guide: per the text of Tax Code Art. 65 (a copy in open sources), the statute of limitations for taxes is 3 years after the end of the tax period; all that time the KGD can review the return. That isn’t the retention period: don’t throw away past years’ documents.

Typical mistakes of a simplified-regime IP

  • Worked on the simplified regime in 2025 but filed no regime notice between Jan 1 and Mar 1, 2026 — per the KGD, such IPs were moved to the general regime (OUR) from Jan 1, 2026.
  • Payment was taken to a personal card or by QR without a fiscal receipt.
  • A zero Form 910.00 was filed, or income shown was less than per the register and bank.
  • A fleet paid by transfer without a receipt, but no ESF was issued.
  • There’s an employee or a GPH contract, but Form 200.00 isn’t filed; the employment contract wasn’t entered into ESUTD within 5 business days (Rules, para. 7, Labor Ministry Order No. 353).
  • A payment went out with the wrong KBK or KNP; in a month with no income, VOSMS for yourself wasn’t paid (per the Kaspi Guide, it’s paid every month).

Key points
  • Before Form 910.00, three checks: a reconciliation with each fleet, register + bank − refunds = income, and a personal account balance of 0.
  • Gaps are traced through documents: a lost act, a payment not in the books, a payment without a receipt, a wrong KBK or KNP.
  • The half-year archive: monthly source documents, reports with receipts, statements, and reconciliation statements; check the retention period with the KGD and don’t throw documents away.
  • Typical mistakes — income bypassing the register, no ESF or Form 200.00, a wrong payment code — are cheaper to find yourself before the KGD asks.

Check yourself

Final test. Which of these is the shop’s income for Form 910.00?
  • Only cash with a receipt
  • Revenue minus spending on parts and rent
  • All money received for work: the register, transfers from companies, even to a personal card — minus refunds

Answer: All money received for work: the register, transfers from companies, even to a personal card — minus refunds — Income on the simplified regime is all money received for work, with no deduction for expenses; only refunds, discounts, and price changes reduce it (lesson 3).

When is Form 910.00 for H2 2026 due, and when is its tax paid?
  • File and pay by Dec 31, 2026
  • File by Feb 15, 2027, pay by Feb 25, 2027
  • File by Jan 15, 2027, pay by Jan 25, 2027

Answer: File by Feb 15, 2027, pay by Feb 25, 2027 — Form 910.00 is filed by the 15th and the tax is paid by the 25th of the second month after the half-year (lessons 2 and 7).

An OPV payment for yourself went out with the VOSMS code by mistake. What will the personal account show?
  • All fine: the money reached the budget
  • Only an OPV overpayment
  • An OPV debt and a VOSMS overpayment

Answer: An OPV debt and a VOSMS overpayment — The money is credited to the payment whose KBK and KNP were given: an OPV debt remains, and interest accrues on it (lessons 4, 5, and 7).
