The Virtual Warehouse

What the Virtual Warehouse is and what’s on its list from 2026, why the product code decides rather than the name, when selling such goods needs an ESF, and how to record their receipt and issue.

The Virtual Warehouse (VW) is the part of the ESF system that tracks the movement of goods on a separate Ministry of Finance list. Whether it affects a repair shop depends on what you buy and sell: oil, fluids, batteries, parts. Let’s see how to check that and what changes in your documents and records.

The list from 2026

From January 1, 2026, the VW list was updated by a Ministry of Finance order of Oct. 31, 2025; sources give the order number differently, No. 661 or No. 681. Per the accounting portal pro1c.kz (Nov. 12, 2025), the list added petroleum products of group 2710 — gasolines, fuel, fuel oil, and others — plus bitumen, propane and butane, and acids.

Tires (code 4011), batteries (8507), and vehicle parts (8708) aren’t among the added items. Whether they’re on the list as a whole isn’t confirmed in the sources we checked: compare by product code or confirm with the KGD. Per the portals, the goods traceability rules were approved by Ministry of Finance Order No. 623 of Oct. 24, 2025.

The code decides, not the name

A product is placed on the list by its code in the commodity nomenclature (the EAEU HS-based TN VED), not by the word on the waybill. “Oil,” “fluid,” or “battery” tells you nothing yet: find the code on the supplier’s ESF or waybill and check the full code against the current list. An oil’s code, for example, may start with 2710, the petroleum products group added to the list.

  1. Take the supplier’s ESF or waybill and find the product code.
  2. Check the full code against the current version of the VW list.
  3. Code not on the list — record the item like a regular part: receipt, issue, balance.
  4. Code on the list — the item’s movement shows in the VW: record receipts and issues only from documents, and confirm how to work with the VW with the KGD.

Selling goods on the list

The general rule (lesson 10): a sale with a KKM receipt or to an individual paying by card needs no ESF. VW goods and imported goods have their own rule. Per the KGD answer of Nov. 13, 2025 (Art. 208(3) of the Tax Code), they can be sold without an ESF only to an individual for personal use or to a micro-business. Any other buyer — for example, a fleet that isn’t a micro-business — needs an ESF. What counts as an imported item in your case — confirm with the KGD.

Records: receipt and issue

Goods on the list are recorded just like parts (course “IP for a repair shop,” the parts lesson): receipt from the supplier’s ESF and waybill, issue from the work order, receipt, or your ESF, balance as the difference; recount the shelf from time to time. The difference is that the movement of such goods also shows in the VW, so every transaction must be backed by a document. And remember: purchases don’t reduce income — Form 910.00 gets the full amount from customers (lesson 3).

Don’t sell a list item to a business client without an ESF, and don’t accept such goods without the supplier’s documents. Not sure whether an item is on the list? Ask the KGD before you sell it.

Key points
  • The VW is the part of the ESF system for goods on a Ministry of Finance list; from 2026 it added petroleum products of group 2710, bitumen, propane and butane, and acids.
  • Tires, batteries, and parts aren’t among the additions; whether an item is on the list depends on its full code, not its name.
  • VW and imported goods go without an ESF only to an individual for personal use or a micro-business (KGD answer, Art. 208(3) of the Tax Code).
  • Receipts and issues of such goods go only by documents; if in doubt, check with the KGD.

How do you tell whether an item is on the VW list?
  • Check the item’s full code from the supplier’s documents against the list
  • By its name on the waybill
  • By its price

Answer: Check the item’s full code from the supplier’s documents against the list — The list is built on product codes. Take the code from the supplier’s ESF or waybill and check it against the current list.
