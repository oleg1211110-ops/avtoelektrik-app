Truck fault codes: SPN/FMI and the J1939 bus

How to read a two-number SPN/FMI code, why 4294967295 is not a fault, and how to check the J1939 bus with a tester. Two simulators: a silent NOx sensor; a broken CAN line.

A code of two numbers: SPN and FMI

On a truck the fault code is usually written as an SPN/FMI pair. SPN is the parameter number: WHAT failed (a sensor, a circuit, a unit). FMI is the failure type: HOW (signal high, low, open, no data). The pair reads the same way on any make — so write the code down in full, both numbers. Example: SPN 3364 is the AdBlue quality sensor; 3364/18 means the quality is below normal.

  • 3 — signal high, 4 — low (more often a short or a broken wire than the sensor itself)
  • 5 — open circuit; 6 — short circuit
  • 2 — implausible readings
  • 7 — not responding; 9 — no data on the bus
  • 15 and 16 — above normal, 17 and 18 — below
  • 31 — a special condition (for example, the AdBlue tank is empty); 4294967295 — “no data”

J1939 — the truck's bus

J1939 is a CAN bus; over it the engine ECU, the NOx sensors, the temperature modules and the sensor in the AdBlue tank talk in numbers. The main rule: “no data” from a sensor is not always the sensor itself. More often it is the power, the connector, the ground or a break in the bus. If another module loads or shorts the bus, there is no communication at all; you find the culprit by disconnecting modules one by one, not by replacing the ECU straight away.

4294967295 is not a fault

If a parameter in the live data shows 4294967295 (that's 0xFFFFFFFF), it means “no data or not supported.” It is not a value and not a fault, and you don't replace a part because of it. And from experience: right after starting, the “downstream” NOx sensor may read 0 — it is waiting for the exhaust to warm up, which is normal.

Checking the J1939 bus with a meter

  • Ignition off: between CAN-H and CAN-L — 54–66 ohms (two 120-ohm terminating resistors in parallel).
  • A break in a bus wire — more than 10 ohms.
  • Ignition on: each wire's voltage to ground is 1–4.5 V (at rest both lines sit at about 2.5 V).
  • The “one by one” check: disconnect sensors and modules in turn; if it returns to normal, the disconnected unit is to blame.

Identify the CAN pins BEFORE testing continuity: find the constant positive and the ground, the remaining two are H and L. Feed power to the bus blindly and you'll kill the bus or a module.

First on your own, then to a specialist

  • Write the code down in full (SPN and FMI) and recall what was done to the vehicle: a repair, a wash, a sensor replacement, whether anyone got into the wiring.
  • “No data”: check the sensor's power fuse, the connector (corrosion, a not-fully-seated plug, water) and the ground.
  • Read the code, clear it, warm the truck up or take it for a drive, and see whether it comes back.
  • If the fuse is intact, the connector is clean, the ground is fine, but the bus is not 54–66 ohms — that's a job for a specialist: ringing out the harness and finding the module loading the bus.
