24 V diesel starting: two batteries, starter, ground

Why a truck won’t start or cranks slowly: two batteries and the link between them, the main positive and the engine ground. Two simulators on the starting circuit.

A diesel starts harder than a petrol engine — high compression, a powerful starter, large cranking currents. So on a truck the starting circuit is checked under load: at rest the voltage may be fine, but sag when you try to start.

Two batteries and the link

The 24 V on a truck comes from two 12 V batteries joined in series by a link. The weak point is the link itself and the terminals: if they corrode or loosen, voltage drops across them under load. The starter ends up cranking weakly even though both batteries are charged.

  • Check both batteries separately — each should hold about 12.6 V.
  • Inspect and wiggle the link and the terminals — check for corrosion and looseness.
  • Measure the voltage under load (while cranking), not only at rest.

Power positive and ground

A thick power cable (positive) runs from the batteries to the starter, and a ground to the engine. A break or a corroded power terminal, and the starter is dead silent. A bad engine ground looks similar: it cranks weakly or just clicks.
