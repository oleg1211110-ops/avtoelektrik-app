SPN 3509 — Sensor 5-volt supply, circuit 1

What the code is made of

SPN 3509Sensor 5-volt supply, circuit 1

OA fault code doesn’t yet mean the unit or sensor itself is faulty: often it’s the plug and connector leading to it.

What you can do yourself

  1. When the computer won’t communicate or there are no 5 volts, it’s not necessarily the computer itself: 5-volt sensors can drag down the 5-volt line, and because of that the computer won’t even switch on.
  2. A short in one sensor pulls down the whole circuit: unplug the sensors one by one until the code goes away.

Stop and don’t drive on if:

  • The Check Engine light is flashing
  • The temperature gauge is in the red or the overheating light is on
  • The red oil pressure light is on
  • Smoke from under the hood, a burning or gas smell
  • Strong jerking, knocking from the engine, or the car barely moves

Add the FMI — the second number of the code. It shows what exactly is wrong with the parameter.

Source: Navistar IK1200516: MaxxForce DT 9/10 DTC (static.nhtsa.gov, MC-10167665)

The second number, FMI: what exactly is wrong

A truck code is two numbers: SPN (which part) and FMI (what is wrong with it). Tap your FMI and the app opens the meaning of that exact code.

  • FMI 0above normal, most severe levelThe sensor sees a real excess. First check the actual parameter (level, temperature, pressure), then the sensor.
  • FMI 1below normal, most severe levelThe sensor sees a real drop. First check the actual parameter, then the sensor.
  • FMI 2erratic, intermittent or incorrectThe signal jumps or doesn’t agree with other sensors: connector, wiring, corroded contacts.
  • FMI 3voltage above normal, shorted to highElectrical: signal wire shorted to positive or open sensor ground. Check the connector and wiring.
  • FMI 4voltage below normal, shorted to lowElectrical: short to ground or no sensor supply. Check the connector, wiring and 5 V supply.
  • FMI 5open circuit, current below normalOpen circuit: connector, wire or the coil of the actuator (valve, injector, relay).
  • FMI 6shorted, current above normalA short in the circuit or a burned-out actuator coil.
  • FMI 7mechanical system not respondingThe actuator doesn’t carry out the command: a stuck valve, actuator or flap.
  • FMI 8abnormal frequencyIncorrect frequency signal: speed or RPM sensor, air gap, wiring, interference.
  • FMI 9data not received in timeThe control unit doesn’t receive data over the CAN bus: power and ground of the sending unit, connectors, bus wires.
  • FMI 10abnormal rate of changeThe signal changes too abruptly: a poor contact in the connector or wiring.
  • FMI 11root cause not knownA scan tool with manufacturer data is needed.
  • FMI 12bad intelligent device or componentA sensor with built-in electronics or a control unit is faulty. Check power and ground, then the component itself.
  • FMI 13out of calibrationThe component must be calibrated (taught) with a scan tool after replacement or repair.
  • FMI 14special instructionsA scan tool with manufacturer data is needed.
  • FMI 15above normal, least severe levelThe value is slightly above normal. Check the actual parameter.
  • FMI 16above normal, moderately severe levelThe value is noticeably above normal. Check the actual parameter, then the sensor.
  • FMI 17below normal, least severe levelThe value is slightly below normal. Check the actual parameter.
  • FMI 18below normal, moderately severe levelThe value is noticeably below normal. Check the actual parameter, then the sensor.
  • FMI 19received network data in errorThe control unit received invalid data over the CAN bus: check the sending unit and the bus wiring.
  • FMI 20reading drifted highThe sensor is ageing and reads high. Compare it with another sensor.
  • FMI 21reading drifted lowThe sensor is ageing and reads low. Compare it with another sensor.
  • FMI 31condition existsA scan tool with manufacturer data is needed.
