Throttle body: how to test it, clean it and do the relearn

The electronic throttle body lets air into the engine on command from the ECU. If it’s dirty, has a poor connection or is faulty, the idle hunts or runs high, the engine stalls when you stop, it responds sluggishly to the gas pedal, the Check Engine light comes on, and the car may go into limp mode: the gas pedal stops responding, and on an automatic the transmission goes into limp mode too. Most often the cause is dirt, wiring or a poor contact.

2. How to test it, step by step

  1. Self-test: remove the intake boot, turn the ignition on without starting the engine and watch the throttle plate — on an electronic throttle it should move slightly. If it doesn’t move, check the ECU fuses, power at the connector, the wiring and the throttle motor.
  2. Look at the throttle plate: carbon, oil and soot around the edge are enough to cause a rough idle. Clean it with a dedicated throttle body cleaner with the ignition off. Don’t force the plate of an electronic throttle open — you can break the drive gears.
  3. Inspect the throttle body connector: any green corrosion, oxidation or moisture on the pins, are the wires intact? Check the battery too: about 12.6 V with the engine off. With low voltage, the electronic throttle and the ECU misbehave.
  4. Check for a poor contact: start the engine and wiggle the throttle body connector and the wires next to it. If the revs jump, the engine stalls or the pedal stops responding, the contact is poor. Clean the pins, tighten the connector terminals and make sure the latch holds.
  5. Connectors are cleaned with a dedicated electrical contact cleaner — it dissolves oxidation and dirt and evaporates quickly, so the contact gets better. For example: Liqui Moly Electronic-Spray, Kontakt 60 (dissolves oxidation; rinse with Kontakt WL afterwards), WD-40 Specialist Contact Cleaner, CRC Contact Cleaner. Regular WD-40 isn’t always right for this: it leaves an oily film that dirt sticks to later. Clean, let it dry, and only then plug it back in.
  6. The throttle position sensor (TPS) runs on a 5 V reference. With the ignition on, a closed throttle usually reads about 0.5 V; open it slowly (on many cars, by pressing the gas pedal) — the voltage should rise smoothly to about 4.5 V with no jumps or dropouts. Glitches mean a bad sensor.
  7. Scan tool: an electronic throttle has two position sensors, and when you press the pedal both signals should change smoothly and in step. P2135 — the two sensor signals disagree (worn unit or connector); P2101, P2111, P2112 — the actuator or a sticking throttle plate.
  8. After cleaning, removing the throttle body or disconnecting the battery, do a relearn. A simple method that doesn’t work on every car: ignition on for 10–30 seconds, off for 10–30 seconds, then start the engine and don’t touch the gas pedal for 5–10 minutes — the ECU relearns the throttle by itself.
  9. A relearn only helps if the throttle is clean and working, with no vacuum leaks and no sensor codes. Common mistakes: revving right after starting, relearning a dirty throttle. If the idle still hunts after the relearn, look for the fault: a relearn doesn’t replace a repair.
Oleg’s tipI don’t replace the throttle body right away: 70 % of the time the problem is dirt, wiring or a poor contact. And after cleaning I always do a relearn — otherwise the idle hunts and the car stalls.

3. Throttle relearn by make

The procedures come only from manufacturers’ bulletins and manuals. Do the pedal and ignition steps exactly on time; if you slip, start over. A relearn only helps if the throttle body is clean and working and there’s no vacuum leak.

Nissan/Infiniti

Pedal and throttle learning — per Nissan bulletin NTB12-035 (2012) for 2004 and later models; idle learning without a scan tool — per the Nissan repair manual (Altima 2007–2012).

  1. Don’t touch the gas pedal. Turn the ignition on for 2 seconds, then off for 10 seconds.
  2. Once more: ignition on for 2 seconds, off for 10 seconds. This is how the ECU learns the released pedal and the closed throttle — during those 10 seconds you can hear the throttle click.
  3. Next — idle learning (Idle Air Volume Learning). Warm up the engine and transmission — about 10 minutes of driving. Battery above 12.9 V at idle, everything off, lever in P or N, steering wheel straight, car stationary.
  4. Turn the ignition off and wait at least 10 seconds.
  5. Pedal released. Turn the ignition on without starting the engine and wait 3 seconds.
  6. Within 5 seconds, quickly press the gas pedal to the floor and release it 5 times.
  7. Wait 7 seconds, press the pedal to the floor and hold it for about 20 seconds — until Check Engine stops flashing and stays on steadily.
  8. Release the pedal no later than 3 seconds after Check Engine comes on steadily.
  9. Start the engine and let it idle for 20 seconds. Rev it 2–3 times and check that the idle is steady.

The pedal steps are strictly timed: use the stopwatch. If you lose track, start over from turning the ignition off.

With a scan tool: CONSULT — Work Support: pedal, throttle closed position and idle learning (IDLE AIR VOL LEARN). In Nissan bulletins from 2013–2017 these learns are done with CONSULT.

Sources: 1, 2, 3

Toyota/Lexus

Per the Toyota repair manual (Prius 2015); other models may differ.

  1. Warm the engine to 80 °C (176 °F) or higher. A/C and everything else off.
  2. Drive at more than 10 km/h (6 mph).
  3. Stop and let it idle for at least 20 seconds.
  4. Repeat the drive and the idle until the idle speed is normal.

According to this manual, disconnecting the battery does not reset the learned values. After cleaning or replacing the throttle body, Toyota first resets them with a scan tool.

With a scan tool: Techstream — Engine and ECT → Utility → Learning Value Reset, then idle learning (ISC Learning).

Sources: 1

Hyundai/Kia

Per a Kia bulletin for Theta 2.0 and 2.4 engines (Optima, Sorento, Sportage, Forte, Rondo). The same engines are used in Hyundai cars, but we didn’t find a separate Hyundai bulletin.

  1. Turn the ignition off and wait 10 seconds.
  2. Turn the ignition on without starting and wait 5 seconds — during this time the ECU learns the throttle. With a Start button: press it twice without pressing the brake and wait about 5 seconds.
  3. Only then start the engine. Start it too early and Check Engine may come on with codes P0638 and P2110: start over.

The bulletin first resets the old learned values with a scan tool, then does the same steps.

With a scan tool: GDS (Hyundai) or KDS (Kia) — Resetting adaptive values.

Sources: 1, 2

Mazda

Per Mazda bulletin 01-025/13 (2013) for the 2010 Mazda5; other models may differ.

  1. Throttle learning: turn the ignition on for 15 seconds without starting, then off for 15 seconds. Repeat 3–5 times.
  2. Start the engine and warm it up until the radiator fan comes on (90 °C (194 °F) or higher).
  3. Idle learning: hold the idle for more than a minute in each mode, in order — in N with no load; in N with the headlights and rear defroster on; in N with the A/C on; in N with the headlights, defroster and A/C on.
  4. Automatic: the same in D, holding the brake with the parking brake on — no load; headlights and defroster; A/C; everything together.

If a scan tool is connected, its test mode must be off — otherwise the learning is reset.

With a scan tool: Mazda IDS (M-MDS) or a multi-brand scan tool with throttle relearn.

Sources: 1

Honda/Acura

Per the Honda repair manual (Jazz/Fit) and a write-up for the 2011 Honda Civic.

  1. Switch everything off: A/C, audio, defroster, headlights.
  2. Turn the ignition on for 2 seconds without starting.
  3. Start the engine and hold 3000 rpm with no load — in P or N — until the radiator fan comes on.
  4. Release the gas and let it idle for about 5 minutes without switching anything on. Don’t count the time while the fan is running.

According to the Honda manual, this relearn is needed after disconnecting the battery, resetting or replacing the ECU, or removing fuses. If you cleared codes with a scan tool, the relearn is needed again too.

With a scan tool: HDS or a multi-brand scan tool — ECM/PCM reset, then the same steps.

Sources: 1, 2

Chevrolet/GM

Per GM service information (V6 LF1 and LFW engines) and Hitachi’s instructions for GM throttle bodies.

  1. Start the engine and let it idle for 3 minutes — automatic in P.
  2. Turn the ignition off for 60 seconds.
  3. Start it again and idle for 3 minutes.
  4. If the idle hasn’t settled, drive above 70 km/h (43 mph) with several lift-offs and long stops at idle, then idle for 5 minutes in P.

During the relearn Check Engine may come on with idle codes — clear them and the ECU will keep learning. After cleaning or replacing the throttle body, GM first resets the learned values with a scan tool.

With a scan tool: GDS2 or Tech2 — Idle Learn Reset, then the same steps.

Sources: 1, 2, 3

Volkswagen/Audi/Skoda/Seat

On VAG cars a throttle adaptation can’t be done without a scan tool.

With a scan tool: VCDS or ODIS — Basic Settings in engine module 01, group 060 (098 on engines with a throttle cable). Ignition on, engine not running, don’t touch the pedal — about 30 seconds.

Sources: 1

Mitsubishi

We found no reliable no-scan-tool procedure in the manufacturer’s public documents — you need a scan tool.

With a scan tool: MUT-III or a multi-brand scan tool with throttle relearn.

Renault

We found no reliable no-scan-tool procedure in the manufacturer’s public documents — you need a scan tool.

With a scan tool: Renault CLIP or a multi-brand scan tool — reset adaptations and relearn the throttle.

Lada

We found no reliable no-scan-tool procedure in AvtoVAZ’s public documents — you need a scan tool.

With a scan tool: diagnostic software for Lada control units with an “adaptation reset” function.

Other makes

Look for the procedure in your car’s repair manual — the sections on throttle or idle learning (throttle learning, idle learn). Can’t find it? You need a scan tool.

With a scan tool: the make’s dealer software or a multi-brand scan tool with throttle relearn.

Look at the throttle plate: carbon, oil and soot around the edge are enough to cause a rough idle. Clean it with a dedicated throttle body cleaner with the ignition off. Don’t force the plate of an electronic throttle open — you can break the drive gears.
