Car fuses: how to check them and why they blow

Sometimes what looks like a serious breakdown is one small fuse: the lights, radio, power windows or heater stop working, or the car won’t start. Check it yourself in a minute — and if it keeps blowing, find out why.

3. How to check a fuse

  1. Turn off the ignition. Find the fuse using the diagram on the fuse box lid, pull it out with the fuse puller and hold it up to the light: if the link inside is broken or darkened, it’s blown. A mini fuse can look fine, so testing with a meter is more reliable.
  2. With a test light, without removing the fuse: clip one lead to ground (bare metal on the body or engine) and touch the probe to each of the two metal test points on top of the fuse in turn. Check ignition-fed circuits with the ignition on; constant-power circuits (hazard lights, alarm) can be checked with it off.
  3. The light is equally bright on both sides — the fuse is good. Only on one side — it’s blown. On neither side — no power is reaching it, keep looking upstream. Noticeably different brightness — a poor contact or corrosion in the fuse box.
  4. With a multimeter: pull the fuse, set continuity mode or resistance on the 200 Ω range, and put the probes on both blades. A beep or about 0 Ω — it’s good; “1” or OL on the display — it’s blown. Without removing it, in DC voltage mode: there should be about 12 V on both sides.

4. Fuse keeps blowing?

A fuse blows on an overload or a short, protecting the wiring and control modules — it’s the result, not the cause.

  1. Replace it only with the same rating — it will be the same color. If a new one blows right away, there’s a short in the circuit or a faulty load: inspect the wiring and connectors for corrosion, and check the relay and the pump, motor or module itself.
  2. If the fuse or its holder gets hot, smells of burnt plastic or the body has melted, the cause is a poor contact, an overload or a poor-quality fuse. Inspect the socket: if it’s darkened or melted, it needs repair — just fitting a new fuse isn’t enough.
  3. Wire add-on equipment — LED headlights, audio, amplifiers, inverters, a parking heater — through a relay and its own fuse. Without a relay the circuit is overloaded and heats up.

5. Where the fuse box is

There are usually two: inside the cabin under the dashboard on the driver’s side, and under the hood next to the battery. Many European and premium cars have a third one in the trunk.

  1. Cabin: look under the steering column by the driver’s knees, on the end of the dashboard on the driver’s side (visible with the door open), left or right of the instrument cluster, and in or behind the glovebox. The box has a plastic cover held by clips.
  2. Under the hood: look for a box with a lid next to the battery, by the fender or near the engine control module. This one holds the high-current fuses and the relays.
  3. Trunk: an extra box may sit behind a side trim panel or next to the battery if it’s mounted at the rear. This is common on European and premium cars.
  4. Can’t find it? Look for a cover marked FUSE or with a lightning bolt symbol, and check the owner’s manual. The power distribution box is almost always next to the battery.
  5. The labeled diagram is on the inside of the lid or in the owner’s manual. Take a photo of it with your phone, and keep a fuse puller and spare fuses of your type in the glovebox.

Questions

What color is a 10 amp fuse?

Red. On standard and mini blade fuses: 5 A tan, 7.5 A brown, 10 A red, 15 A blue, 20 A yellow, 25 A clear or white, 30 A green, 40 A orange. The rating is also printed on top.

Can I use a higher amp fuse?

No. Never fit a higher-rated fuse, even “temporarily”, and never bridge it with wire: the wiring will melt, the engine computer may fail or the car may catch fire.

Why does my car fuse keep blowing?

A fuse blows on an overload or a short — it’s the result, not the cause. If a new one blows right away, there’s a short in the circuit or a faulty load: inspect the wiring and connectors, then check the relay and the pump, motor or module itself.

How do I know if a fuse is blown?

Pull it and hold it up to the light: a broken or darkened link means it’s blown. A mini fuse can look fine, so a test light or multimeter is more reliable: power on both test points means it’s good, on one side only means it’s blown.

Where is the fuse box in my car?
