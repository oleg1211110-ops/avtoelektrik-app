Car stalls at idle, when hot or while driving: what to check yourself

First, pin down when it stalls: right after starting, when coming to a stop, when hot, or while driving — and remember what was done to the car recently. Each case has its own usual suspects: the immobilizer, the fuel pump, a vacuum leak, a dirty throttle body, the crankshaft sensor.

2. Starts and dies right away

  1. If it stalls after 1–3 seconds and the key symbol flashes, try the second key: that’s how the immobilizer blocks it.
  2. Check the fuses and listen for whether the fuel pump runs.
  3. Inspect the plugs and connectors, and check the battery and terminals.
  4. If it started after a battery terminal was disconnected, the cause may be a dirty throttle plate: it needs cleaning and adaptation.
  5. If it started after a throttle body cleaning or other intake work, check that all the hoses and pipes are in place.

3. Stalls at idle, the idle hunts

A common cause of a rough idle is a vacuum leak: extra air gets into the intake bypassing the mass air flow sensor (MAF) or downstream of the throttle — through a cracked intake hose, a vacuum line, the intake manifold gasket or the PCV valve. Signs: the idle hunts or jumps, the engine runs rough at idle, and lean codes P0171 and P0174 may set.

  1. If the idle went high or started hunting right after cleaning the throttle body or disconnecting the battery, run a throttle adaptation and idle relearn: the ECU remembers the corrections for the dirty throttle. On many cars this can be done without a scan tool — see the “Throttle relearn by make” button below.
  2. Connect a scan tool and find intake manifold pressure (the MAP sensor). With the engine off and the ignition on it equals atmospheric pressure — about 900 mbar (90 kPa (13.1 psi)). The exact figure depends on where you are: the higher above sea level, the lower it is.
  3. Start the engine and let it warm up. At idle intake pressure drops to roughly 300–400 mbar (30–40 kPa (4.4–5.8 psi)). Noticeably higher and wandering — suspect a vacuum leak; misfires or off timing can look the same.
  4. Check the fuel trims: on a warm idle the sum of short- and long-term trim goes above +10…+15 % and drops at higher rpm — the typical picture of a vacuum leak.
  5. A smoke machine finds the leak most precisely: smoke is fed into the intake and comes out wherever it isn’t sealed.
  6. Without a smoke machine: with the engine running, lightly spray carburetor cleaner along the intake joints — from the MAF along the hose to the throttle body, along the intake manifold gasket and the vacuum lines. If the rpm rises for a moment, the leak is near that spot.
  7. Check the obvious: clamps and the intake boot after the MAF, vacuum lines (cracks, popped-off hoses), the brake booster hose, the PCV valve, the oil filler cap and the dipstick.
Oleg’s tipI check for a vacuum leak with the scan tool first: with the engine off intake pressure is about 900 — that’s atmospheric; with it running, 300–400. If it’s higher at idle, I look for the leak with a smoke machine, and if I don’t have one handy I lightly spray carb cleaner along the intake from the MAF. If the rpm picks up, the leak is roughly there.

4. Stalls when hot or while driving

Driving is dangerousDon’t drive with a faulty crankshaft sensor: the engine can stall at any moment, including while driving, and may not restart.
  1. Crank the engine and watch the tachometer and the rpm in the scan tool live data: while cranking you normally see about 150–300 rpm. If it stays at zero, the ECU isn’t getting a crank signal. Sensor codes: P0335 — circuit, P0336 — signal out of range.
  2. Find the sensor: it is usually near the crankshaft pulley, on the engine block, or by the flywheel on the transmission side. Inspect the connector (corrosion) and wires (breaks, chafing). Remove the sensor and clean the tip of oil, dirt and metal shavings.
  3. Does the engine stall when hot and refuse to start, but start again once it cools down? Suspect the crankshaft sensor: they often fail when hot. Test right after it quits, while hot: the cranking rpm on the scan tool and the sensor resistance.
Oleg’s tipVery often the problem isn’t the sensor itself but corroded contacts, damaged wiring or a poor ground. So I don’t rush to replace the sensor — first I check everything step by step.

Fuel pump: when you turn the key to ON, the pump hums for 2–3 seconds and builds pressure. If it’s silent, it isn’t getting power or it has seized. Have a helper switch on the ignition while you knock with your fist or a rubber mallet on the bottom of the tank. On many cars the pump sits under the rear seat: lift the seat, open the hatch and tap the pump cover. If the pump starts humming and the car starts, it is worn — replace it soon.

    What is it?

    Questions

    Why does the engine start and then die right away?

    The engine starts and stalls after a couple of seconds. The cause can be the immobilizer, the fuel supply, the throttle body or a bad contact. If it stalls after 1–3 seconds and the key symbol flashes, try the second key: that’s how the immobilizer blocks it. If it started after a battery terminal was disconnected, the cause may be a dirty throttle plate: it needs cleaning and adaptation.

    Why does my car stall at idle or when I come to a stop?

    A common cause of a rough idle is a vacuum leak: extra air gets into the intake bypassing the mass air flow sensor (MAF) or downstream of the throttle — through a cracked intake hose, a vacuum line, the intake manifold gasket or the PCV valve. Signs: the idle hunts or jumps, the engine runs rough at idle, and lean codes P0171 and P0174 may set.

    It stalls when hot and starts again once it cools down. What is it?

    Does the engine stall when hot and refuse to start, but start again once it cools down? Suspect the crankshaft sensor: they often fail when hot. Test right after it quits, while hot: the cranking rpm on the scan tool and the sensor resistance.

    It stalls after the battery was disconnected or the throttle body cleaned — what now?

    After cleaning or replacing the throttle body, disconnecting the battery or replacing the engine control unit, the idle can be high or hunt, and the engine may stall when you stop. The ECU still remembers the corrections for the dirty throttle, or it has lost its learned values — it needs to relearn. On many cars you can do this yourself, without a scan tool: each make has its own procedure — see below.

    Is it safe to drive if the car stalls while driving?

    Don’t drive with a faulty crankshaft sensor: the engine can stall at any moment, including while driving, and may not restart. Find the cause before any long trips: check the crankshaft sensor, the fuel pump and the trouble codes.

    It stalls while driving but runs fine after a restart — why?

    Most often the crankshaft sensor or the fuel pump. Listen for the pump humming for 2–3 seconds when you turn the key to ON, and read the codes: P0335/P0336 — the crankshaft sensor, P0300–P0308 — misfires.
