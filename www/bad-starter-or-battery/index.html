Bad starter or bad battery? Tell by the sound

The sound tells most of it. Rapid clicking and a dimming dash — usually the battery or its terminals. One loud click and nothing turns — usually the starter. Auto electrician Oleg adds one more: a failing starter can draw so much current that the dash dims just like with a dead battery.

Practice: find the cause

A car won’t start. Run the checks in any order, then name the cause.

    What is it?

    2. What to check yourself

    1. Turn the key (or press Start) and listen. Watch the dash at the same time: does it dim, and how much?
    2. Check the battery terminals: tight, no white or blue-green crust — how to clean them.
    3. Measure the battery while someone cranks: it should stay above about 9.5–10 V. Lower — the battery is weak or discharged (test it, or jump start it).
    4. The battery holds up but nothing turns — check the starter fuse and relay (swap the relay with a neighboring one with the same marking), and the shifter: fully in Park, or try Neutral; on a manual, press the clutch all the way down.
    5. One click and nothing: with the parking brake on and the car in Park or Neutral, have someone hold the key in Start and, at that very moment, lightly tap the starter body. Tapping without the key in Start is useless. If it cranks — the starter is failing.
    6. A charged battery collapses while cranking and a jump doesn’t help — the starter may be drawing far too much current. That’s a starter repair.
    Tapping the starter: only with the key in Start — and carefullyTap at the moment the key is held in Start; at any other moment it does nothing. Parking brake on, the car in Park or Neutral, hands and tools away from the belts and the fan. If you can’t reach the starter safely, don’t.

    3. Bad starter symptoms

    • One click and no crank, with the dash staying bright.
    • It starts only after a few tries or after tapping the starter.
    • Slow, labored cranking with a charged battery.
    • Whirring without the engine turning, or grinding.
    • Smoke or a burning smell from under the car after cranking — stop trying: the starter or its cable is overheating.

    Questions

    How do I know if it’s the starter or the battery?

    Listen and watch the dash while you turn the key. Rapid clicking and a dash that dims — the battery or its terminals. One loud click, the dash stays bright, the engine doesn’t turn — the starter or its circuit. Measure the battery while someone cranks: it should stay above about 9.5–10 V.

    Can a bad starter make the dash lights dim?

    Yes. In auto electrician Oleg’s experience, a faulty starter can draw far too much current — the dash dims strongly, just like with a weak battery. If the battery is charged and a jump start doesn’t help, suspect the starter.

    Does tapping the starter work?

    Sometimes — on worn brushes or a sticking solenoid. But only at the right moment: someone holds the key in Start, and you tap the starter body right then. Tapping it without the key in Start does nothing, auto electrician Oleg stresses. If it cranks, the starter is failing; it isn’t a fix. Keep away from the belts and the fan, with the parking brake on and the car in Park or Neutral.

    Why does my car make no sound at all when I turn the key?

    Check that the shifter is fully in Park or Neutral (or the clutch pedal fully down), the starter fuse and relay, and the key: a flashing security light means the immobilizer doesn’t see the key. On some cars with a deeply discharged battery the computer won’t even send the start command.

    The starter spins but the engine doesn’t turn — what is it?

    A whirring without the engine turning means the starter drive (the gear that engages the flywheel) isn’t engaging. A grinding noise — worn teeth on the starter gear or the flywheel. Both need a repair.
