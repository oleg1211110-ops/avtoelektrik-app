2. Is the battery worn out?

A battery usually lasts 3–5 years. An old one may show normal voltage with no load but sag when it cranks the starter.

  1. Look for the manufacturing date on the case or label: older than 4–5 years is already a reason to test it.
  2. Charge the battery with a charger and test again: if the voltage drops quickly or the starter cranks slowly again, it’s time to replace the battery.
  3. A swollen case, cracks or electrolyte leaks — replace it at once.
  4. Many auto parts stores, battery shops and auto electricians will load-test a battery.
  5. Choose the new battery to match the original’s capacity, cold-cranking amps (CCA) and terminal layout. On cars with start-stop, use only the same type: AGM or EFB.
  6. On many newer cars (BMW, Audi, VW, Mercedes and others) a new battery is registered with a scan tool — otherwise the car will charge it incorrectly. Replacing an AGM battery? Do the “battery replacement” reset, or the new battery will be overcharged.

3. Battery keeps dying? Find the drain

When the battery goes flat overnight or within a few days, usually some module doesn’t go to sleep. Most often it’s equipment that wasn’t fitted at the factory: an alarm or remote start, a stereo, a dash cam, a tracker. Start there.

While the meter is connected in seriesdon’t turn on the ignition or start the engine — the meter will burn out.
  1. Unplug everything powered from the cigarette lighter and USB overnight: dash cam, chargers, tracker. If the battery is fine in the morning, the culprit is one of them.
  2. Aftermarket stereo: turn off the ignition and lock the car. The stereo and its button lights should go dark. If they don’t, it was wired to constant power.
  3. Check the lights that should switch off on their own: glovebox, trunk, dome lights, vanity mirrors. Can’t see the glovebox light? Put your phone inside recording video, close it for a minute, then watch.
  4. Measure the parasitic draw. Turn off the ignition and everything else, close the doors. Remove the negative terminal, set the multimeter to DC amps with the red lead in the 10 A jack, and connect it between the battery’s negative post and the removed terminal.
  5. Wait for the modules to go to sleep — from a few minutes to an hour — without opening the doors. Ideally the draw is 0.06 A (60 mA) or less; 0.1–0.2 A or more means something isn’t going to sleep.
  6. Pull the fuses one at a time: the one that makes the current drop is on the circuit with the drain. Sometimes a faulty alternator is what draws the current.

Questions

What voltage should a car battery have?

With the engine off and everything switched off, 1–2 hours after a drive: 12.6–12.8 V is charged, about 12.4 V is roughly 75 %, 12.2 V is half, and 12.0 V and below is flat.

How low should a battery drop when cranking?

A healthy battery stays at 9.5–10 V or above while the starter cranks. Lower means it is weak or discharged.

How long does a car battery last?

Usually 3–5 years. An old battery may show normal voltage with no load but sag when it cranks the starter, so test it while cranking.

Why does my car battery keep dying?

Usually one of three causes: a parasitic drain (something doesn’t go to sleep when the car is parked), the alternator isn’t charging, or the battery has reached the end of its life.

Will driving recharge a dead battery?

Not fully. Charge it with a battery charger, then test it again.

What voltage is normal with the engine running?

13.5–14.8 V at the battery terminals. On newer cars with “smart” charging or start-stop, 12.5–15 V is normal as long as there are no fault codes and the battery light is off.
